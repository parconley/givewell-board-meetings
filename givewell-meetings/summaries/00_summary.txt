The Clear Fund Board of Directors convened on June 22, 2007, for a foundational meeting that established critical organizational structures, strategic direction, and operational parameters for what would become GiveWell. All six directors attended: Bob Elliott, Virginia Zink, Holden Karnofsky, Lucy Bernholz, Greg Jensen, and Tim Ogden, along with Program Officer Elie Hassenfeld.

Strategic Scope and Grant Application Process

The board's primary focus centered on determining the organization's scope for 2007, specifically addressing which charitable causes the Clear Fund would evaluate. The organization faced an unexpectedly strong response from potential grant applicants, with approximately 300 organizations (out of 1,000 contacted) expressing interest. These applicants spanned five core cause areas: saving lives in the developing world (approximately 75 organizations), economic opportunity in the developing world (45 organizations), educational opportunity in NYC (70 organizations), preschool in NYC (25 organizations), and economic opportunity for adults in NYC (50 organizations).

Holden Karnofsky presented several options for managing this substantial applicant pool, with his preferred approach being a two-step grant application process that would systematically narrow the field. After extensive discussion, the Board unanimously approved an ambitious timeline and methodology at 7:46pm. The approved plan established a rigorous multi-stage evaluation process: within one week, preliminary applications would be distributed requesting data on specific programs for at least 4 of the 5 causes, with applicants given 4 weeks to respond. The organization would then respond on a rolling basis, limiting full applications to only 10 charities per cause while publicly posting reasoning for eliminations. Full applications would be given 6 weeks, with evaluation beginning by September 15 and a hard deadline of October 31 for back-and-forth questioning.

The plan called for reducing applicants to 10 per cause, writing full reviews of 5 per cause by Thanksgiving, holding a board meeting the week after Thanksgiving, and distributing grants in early December. Site visits would be conducted for probable winners of each NYC cause, with additional visits if initial impressions were unfavorable, plus at least one on-campus recruiting trip to build organizational capacity.

Organizational Guiding Principles

The board established four foundational principles that would guide charity selection. First, the organization focused on direct humanitarian aid, seeking to translate monetary donations into improved lives without reliance on advocacy, research, or public education campaigns. Second, the Clear Fund emphasized "enabling lives of full opportunity," targeting assistance to people with potential for full participation in society while intentionally filtering out charities focused on the elderly and infirm or the terminally ill and irrevocably disabled. Third, a geographical focus on NYC and Africa was established. Fourth, scalability was prioritized, with preference given to larger charities with existing activities capable of expanding with additional funding.

The organization's outreach strategy had been comprehensive, involving the purchase of 3,502 records from GuideStar, filtered by NTEE classification, budget size, and geographical region. Staff manually reviewed mission statements and accomplishments, ultimately mailing letters to 1,023 addresses (663 domestic, 113 international), with 33 returned as undeliverable as of June 21, 2007.

Personnel and Governance Decisions

Virginia Zink was formally and unanimously elected as Board Vice President and Treasurer at 7:52pm, establishing key leadership structure for the nascent organization.

Compensation decisions represented a significant portion of the meeting's deliberations. The board reviewed extensive comparability data for Executive Director and Program Officer positions. Holden Karnofsky had consulted with Jason McGill of the New York Regional Association of Grantmakers to identify comparable organizations, including the New York Women's Foundation (Executive Director compensation of $129,231 for total expenses of $3,023,293), Third Wave Foundation (Executive Director compensation of $51,827 for total expenses of $657,468), and North Star Fund (Executive Director compensation of $67,015 for total expenses of $1,072,280).

Additional salary data from the Nonprofit Coordinating Committee of New York indicated that for nonprofits in health and human services with annual budgets between $400,000 and $700,000, median Executive Director salary was $68,000 annually, with Deputy Director at $48,000. For public societal benefit organizations in the same budget range, median Executive Director salary was $74,000.

Holden Karnofsky was excused from the room at 8:09pm while the board discussed proposed compensation for him and Elie Hassenfeld. At 8:10pm, the board approved annualized salaries of $68,600 (including benefits) for both Holden Karnofsky as Executive Director and Elie Hassenfeld as Program Officer. This compensation was set acknowledging that Karnofsky expected to work approximately 80 hours per week and Hassenfeld expected to work in excess of 60 hours per week, making the effective hourly rates considerably below the nominal annual figures suggested by comparable organizations.

Financial Planning and Budget Approval

The Clear Fund's 2007 budget was set at approximately $250,000, translating to approximately $500,000 on an annualized basis. Formal commitments to donate totaled $251,000, with $17,800 already received as of the meeting date.

The proposed budget was approved at 8:22pm with several significant modifications and caveats. A planned trip to Africa was eliminated from the budget. The Executive Director was granted authority to make minor budget changes while being held accountable at later meetings. The budget was explicitly limited to calendar year 2007 only, with future plans contingent on December receipts and subject to discussion at a later board meeting. The board acknowledged the realistic possibility that the project might be placed on hold after website construction in December, demonstrating a measured approach to the organization's sustainability and growth.

The budget notably allocated approximately $5,000 for accounting firm assistance with initial setup, while strategically planning to stay under $250,000 in gross receipts for 2007 to maintain the option of performing less than a full audit. Individual grant sizes remained indeterminate but were provisionally set at $20,000 for planning purposes.

Governance and Compliance Framework

The board established comprehensive conflict of interest and compensation approval policies aligned with IRS regulations. All directors signed annual statements affirming receipt, understanding, and agreement to comply with conflict of interest policies, and acknowledging the organization's charitable status and tax-exempt purpose requirements. These policies included detailed procedures for disclosure, determining conflicts of interest, compensation approval processes, and periodic reviews to ensure compliance with charitable purposes.

The meeting concluded at 8:24pm, with the next meeting scheduled for the week after Thanksgiving, positioning the board to review progress on the ambitious grant evaluation timeline and make final grant decisions before year-end.
