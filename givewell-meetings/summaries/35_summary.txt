Episode 35: Board Meeting - October 30, 2017

Meeting Details:
- Date: October 30, 2017
- Duration: 1 hour 32 minutes
- Format: Conference call
- Chair: Tom Rutledge

Summary:

This comprehensive fall board meeting marked a strategic inflection point for GiveWell as the organization assessed its progress and charted an ambitious course forward across research, operations, and outreach.

State of the Organization:

The meeting opened with Elie Hassenfeld's presentation on the "State of GiveWell," highlighting that the research function was in the best shape in the organization's history. Senior Research Analysts Natalie Crispin and Josh Rosenberg were effectively managing nearly all day-to-day research operations, having grown substantially in their roles over 8 and 4 years respectively. The research team was producing higher quality work while covering more ground than ever before, with top charities collectively showing more room for additional funding than at any previous point.

Operations had evolved from a highly problematic area to a functioning department over the preceding 18 months. Under the leadership of Director of Operations Sarah Ward, hired in mid-2016, the team had established proper structure and domain ownership. However, significant improvements were still needed in metrics assessment, money moved reporting, budgeting capabilities, and HR functions.

Strategy Review and Future Direction:

The board engaged with GiveWell's August 2017 strategy review, which outlined the organization's vision and priorities across its three main domains. The strategy document reaffirmed GiveWell's mission to find and support the development of outstanding giving opportunities while publishing full analytical details to help donors make informed decisions.

A major strategic shift was the elevation of donor outreach to a top organizational priority. GiveWell set an ambitious goal to raise an additional $60-100 million in annual money moved (excluding Good Ventures) by giving season 2022—a five-year horizon. This represented a significant departure from the organization's historically limited investment in proactive outreach efforts.

Elie's top three priorities as Executive Director were clearly articulated:
1. Maximize long-term staff impact, especially direct reports, through coaching, mentorship, and leadership development
2. Improve organizational culture, specifically around responding to ideas/critiques and providing clearer performance feedback
3. Develop and execute an effective outreach strategy to substantially increase money moved

Financial Management and Governance:

The board reviewed GiveWell's budgeting process with the goal of reducing the management time required for budget updates while maintaining appropriate financial oversight. A significant discussion centered on the application of the Excess Assets Policy, which was designed to communicate to donors GiveWell's intent to grant out excess unrestricted assets to recommended charities.

After careful analysis of current unrestricted assets relative to the policy guidelines, the board determined that GiveWell had no excess assets requiring earmarking for regranting at that time. This reflected the organization's growth trajectory and increased operational needs.

Board Structure and Governance Enhancements:

The meeting included approval of minutes from the June 6, 2017 meeting, which had addressed important governance matters including Board member resignations (Jake Gibson and Phil Steinmeyer) and the reduction of Board size from eleven to nine members to improve operational efficiency.

A significant governance enhancement was the formation of a Finance Subcommittee to provide specialized oversight of financial matters. The subcommittee was composed of Timothy Ogden (Chair), Rob Reich, and Julia Wise. This represented part of GiveWell's maturing governance structure with specialized committees handling specific oversight domains including Finance, Audit, and Strategy.

Conflict of Interest Management:

Sarah Ward led the board through a review of prior Open Philanthropy Project transactions that raised actual or apparent conflicts of interest. These conflicts had been identified through annual conflict of interest declarations submitted during Summer 2017. The board engaged in thorough discussion of the transactions and the conflicts of interest involved, demonstrating GiveWell's commitment to transparency and proper conflict management given the interconnected relationships between GiveWell, Good Ventures, and the Open Philanthropy Project.

The review underscored the importance of formal processes for identifying and addressing potential conflicts, particularly as GiveWell's ecosystem included board members who served in multiple capacities across related organizations.

Operational Excellence:

Throughout the meeting, there was clear recognition that while GiveWell had made substantial progress in professionalizing its operations, continued improvement was necessary. The focus on building proper systems, metrics, and accountability mechanisms reflected an organization transitioning from startup mode to a more mature operational model.

The operations team structure was mostly in place, but work remained on developing effective metrics and assessment systems to enable strategic priority-setting and performance tracking. Specific areas identified for improvement included:
- Accurate reporting of money moved metrics
- Enhanced budgeting capabilities
- Strengthened HR department functionality
- Better donations processing systems

Looking Forward:

The meeting concluded with acknowledgment that a planned discussion regarding further Board size adjustments would be postponed to the next meeting due to time constraints. This reflected the substantial agenda and the board's commitment to giving adequate time to important governance decisions.

The tone throughout the meeting was one of cautious optimism—recognition of significant progress made while maintaining clear-eyed awareness of the work ahead. GiveWell was positioned at an important juncture, having built a solid foundation in research and operations while preparing to substantially scale its outreach efforts to maximize impact on global health and poverty alleviation.

The meeting demonstrated mature organizational governance, with appropriate board oversight of strategy, finance, conflicts of interest, and operational performance while empowering management to execute on the organization's ambitious mission.
