           GiveWell's Financial Situation as of December 15, 2011

Summary

    • GiveWell intends to expand substantially over the coming year. We  are
      having increasing influence and increasing research depth, as well  as
      launching  a  new  initiative  (GiveWell  Labs)   that   will   create
      substantially more work. We believe we can productively use more staff
      to increase our capacity.
    • Based on current projections, GiveWell projects  a  small  surplus  in
      2012, and has a reasonable level of cash-on-hand. In the  short  term,
      we can expand without needing to identify new revenue sources.
    • GiveWell will need to fundraise and find new revenue sources in  order
      to maintain the size we hope to reach in mid-2012. Our projected  2013
      deficit (based on current revenue sources) is $100,000-$200,000.
    • GiveWell has an  "excess  assets"  policy,  specifying  that  once  we
      surpass a certain level of  unrestricted  assets,  we  grant  out  the
      excess to top charities rather than continue  to  hold  it  ourselves.
      This policy is intended to ensure that donors  to  GiveWell  need  not
      worry about our room for more funding: when we have more than  we  can
      productively use ourselves, the result is  more  grants  to  the  best
      charities we've found.

The case for expansion

Our view, which we will discuss in more detail when doing our formal  annual
self-evaluation in January 2011, is that GiveWell should be expanding.

    • Our influence is growing rapidly.
         o Donations to top charities tracked through the  website  are  up
           about 200-300% in 2011 vs. 2010 (and in 2010 they were up  about
           200% vs. 2009). For 2011, we project well  over  $1  million  in
           donations through the website and well over $2 million in  total
           donations to our top charities, counting only  donors  who  give
           $250,000 and under (larger donors  are  now  being  tracked  and
           dealt with  differently,  as  discussed  under  "GiveWell  Labs"
           below). Given that we are still growing significantly,  that  we
           have stuck to only a basic level of outreach efforts,  and  that
           GiveWell-using donors are substantially younger than  a  typical
           donor base, we expect these figures to rise significantly in the
           future.
         o Growth is robust: there has  also  been  strong  growth  in  the
           number of donations (also up 200-300% in  2011  vs.  2010  after
           only growing ~50% between 2010 and  2009).  Website  traffic  is
           also up significantly year over year ( > 100% growth in 2011 vs.
           2010).
         o We have also gained credibility among academics  and  funders  -
           partly because  of  our  growing  impact  on  donations,  partly
           because of our improved network, and partly because of  specific
           work we've done (for example, publishing substantial analysis of
           the funding needs for the March 2011 relief effort in Japan  and
           finding  major  errors  in  World  Health   Organization   cost-
           effectiveness estimates).
    • Our access is improving. Because of the  above,  our  ability  to  get
      charities and scholars to engage  with  us  is  improving,  giving  us
      access to a lot more information that we need the capacity to  analyze
      and incorporate into our research.
    • Our level of depth is increasing. For our 2011  top  charities,  we've
      done far  more  investigation  and  analysis  than  for  previous  top
      charities, including  extended  pre-recommendation  field  visits  and
      detailed original cost-effectiveness analysis (we no  longer  rely  on
      World Health Organization estimates now that we've  found  substantial
      problems with them). We find this additional analysis to be  important
      in making the best recommendation possible,  which  is  becoming  more
      important with our higher profile and greater impact on donations.
    • GiveWell Labs is a promising initiative that will create  a  lot  more
      work. In addition to seeing growing  influence  among  $250k-and-under
      donors, we now have relationships with a few people who are capable of
      giving  greater  amounts,  and  we   have   launched   GiveWell   Labs
      (http://blog.givewell.org/category/givewell-labs/)  to  better   serve
      this audience (as well as  to  find  better  giving  opportunities  in
      general). $1 million has been pre-committed to  this  initiative.  The
      research process will be distinct from (though will have some  overlap
      with) our core work  of  finding  great  charities,  and  may  involve
      investigations into many causes we haven't previously covered.
    • Our ability to hire and manage new talent is  improving.  All  of  the
      above point to a need for more capacity. Fortunately, we feel we  have
      developed good processes for identifying and managing new  talent.  In
      the summer of 2011,  we  worked  productively  with  a  7-person  team
      including two temporary hires; we are looking to get back up  to,  and
      perhaps a bit above, this staff size. As of mid-January 2011, we  will
      have 5 people on staff, all with strong and substantial track  records
      of contributing to GiveWell.

Needs after 2012

We project a small surplus  over  2012,  but  2013  projected  expenses  are
$584,355.57, more if we want to continue expanding ($584,355.57 is based  on
a staff size of 8, which we hope to reach around mid-2012). Because not  all
of the 2012 revenue is  expected  to  recur  in  the  future  (as  discussed
above), we have a projected gap of $100,000-$200,000 (depending  on  whether
we retain the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation's support) that  we  need
to close by then to avoid deficits.

Thus, while we consider ourselves to be in a strong financial position  with
the ability to expand as warranted, it is also necessary for us to  continue
to fundraise and identify new revenue sources.

"Excess assets" policy

GiveWell has an "excess assets" policy, specifying that once  we  surpass  a
certain level of unrestricted  assets,  we  grant  out  the  excess  to  top
charities rather  than  continue  to  hold  it  ourselves.  This  policy  is
intended to ensure that donors to GiveWell need not  worry  about  our  room
for more funding: when we have more than we can productively use  ourselves,
the result is more grants to the best charities we've found.

We seek to hold a reasonable level of assets: one that gives  us  a  stable,
predictable financial situation and the ability to  plan  and  to  take  any
outstanding  opportunities  that  come  up  (particularly  with  regard   to
hiring). Assets beyond what we feel we need in order to accomplish this  are
granted        out.         Details         are         available         at
http://www.givewell.org/about/official-records/Excess-Assets-Policy

                 GiveWell Progress Report, mid-December 2011

Since our last  Board  meeting,  we  have  been  overwhelmingly  focused  on
finding and researching top charities.

We published our new recommendations and research on Tuesday,  November  29.
We have two top charities, both of  which  we  have  researched  in  greater
depth than any previous recommended charity, and both of which  we  consider
outstanding.

Since then, we have been focused on optimizing our website  in  response  to
increased traffic and feedback, as well as reaching out to past  donors  for
updates and discussions. We are aiming for daily blog posts through the  end
of the calendar year, and will be focusing on these  as  well  as  continued
donor relations/maintenance until January, at which point we will begin  our
annual self-evaluation.

Growth in influence continues to be strong:
    • Donations tracked directly through  the  website  continue  to  be  up
      between 300% and 400% over 2010 (this holds for December  specifically
      as well as for the rest of the year). As of 11a on December 15,  2011,
      we had tracked $543,115 through the website and $1,383,747 in  overall
      money moved.
    • Because of the fact that donations tend to be concentrated at the  end
      of December (particularly the 31st), we  are  currently  projecting  a
      2011 total of about $1.4 million  through  the  website  (compared  to
      $400,000 in 2010) and about $2.5 million  overall  (compared  to  $1.6
      million in 2010). The latter figure excludes $1 million  in  committed
      funding for GiveWell Labs.
    • Growth is robust: there has also been strong growth in the  number  of
      donations (also up 200-300% in 2011 vs. 2010 after only  growing  ~50%
      between 2010 and 2009). Website traffic is also up significantly  year
      over year ( > 100% growth in 2011 vs. 2010).

We intend to make GiveWell Labs a major focus of our work after the new
year.
