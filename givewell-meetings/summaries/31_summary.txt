GIVEWELL BOARD MEETING #33 - JUNE 14, 2016

BOARD RE-ELECTION

The board voted to re-elect all current board members. The full board at this time consisted of: Elie Hassenfeld, Holden Karnofsky (Secretary), Tim Ogden (Vice-Chair), Tom Rutledge (Chair), Cari Tuna (Treasurer), plus Jake Gibson, Greg Jensen, Rob Reich, Brigid Slipka, Phil Steinmeyer, and Julia Wise. This represented the full complement of eleven board members allowed under the bylaws.

BUDGET AND FINANCIAL POSITION

The board engaged in extensive 33-minute discussion of the proposed 2016 budget and proposed budget for the first half of 2017. As of April 2016, Givewell projected 12-month forward expenses of $6.4 million and revenues of $6.5 million, holding $4.3 million in reserves. This represented substantial growth from previous years as both traditional GiveWell work and the Open Philanthropy Project continued expanding.

GiveWell's unrestricted revenue increased by $1.5 million in 2015, from $3.0 million in 2014 to $4.5 million in 2015. The growth drivers included: Good Ventures increasing unrestricted support by $800,000 (from $1.2 million to $2.0 million), partly due to shifting from restricted to unrestricted funding and partly due to increasing support for Open Philanthropy Project growth; three new donors contributing $400,000; donors giving less than $10,000 per year increasing support by $200,000 (from $500,000 to $700,000); while other donors collectively gave the same amount in 2015 as 2014.

The Open Philanthropy Project now accounted for the majority of GiveWell's expenses, estimated at approximately $4.7 million of the $6.4 million in projected expenses over the next 12 months. GiveWell intended to separate the Open Philanthropy Project into a separate organization by the end of 2016, no longer supported by GiveWell's unrestricted funding.

Under the planned separation, GiveWell would tentatively ask Good Ventures to fund 100% of Open Philanthropy Project expenses (rather than the previous 50%) and continue to support 20% of GiveWell's expenses. Given this anticipated change, GiveWell believed it was in a strong financial position with $4.3 million in reserves and expected $2.2 million from Good Ventures in the next 1-2 months.

Revenue projections for May 2016 through April 2017 totaled $6.5 million based on: Good Ventures funding 20% of GiveWell expenses and 100% of Open Philanthropy expenses at $2.4 million ($2.2 million grant plus $200,000 in donated rent, assuming Open Philanthropy Project separated by September 2016); major supporters (donors giving $10,000 or more) at $2.8 million ($1.4 million from about twenty very likely continuing donors, $800,000 from other past supporters, $600,000 from new major supporters); $1.0 million from small donors assuming approximately 33% growth from 2015 (following 43% growth in 2015); and $200,000 from other one-off sources.

The board approved the budget for 2016 and the first six months of 2017 following the executive compensation discussion.

EXECUTIVE COMPENSATION

The board discussed and approved increasing co-Executive Director salaries from $150,000 to $175,000 annually (approximately $155,000 to $180,000 in total compensation including health benefits). This represented a $25,000 annual increase from the $150,000 salary approved in June 2015.

The compensation review used the same comparative data categories as the previous year (2015 data, carried forward without update): foundations (private data from 117 foundations surveyed in 2014), research nonprofits (990 data from five organizations in 2013), academics (UC Berkeley economics professors from 2011 data), and social entrepreneurs (data from 2010-2011).

Significantly, the foundation comparison was updated to reflect GiveWell and Open Philanthropy Project's combined approximately $117 million in money moved in 2015 (compared to approximately $36 million in 2014). This substantial increase in influence justified comparison to larger foundations. For foundations with approximately 10-16 staff, CEO total compensation ranged from $304,500 (25th percentile) to $330,000 (median) to $443,400 (75th percentile). For foundations with approximately $100-500 million in assets (assumed to spend 5% annually, moving approximately $5-25 million per year), CEO compensation showed similar ranges.

Holden and Elie took questions on the proposed compensation, then left the room for board deliberation and vote by unconflicted members. The proposed $175,000 salary remained well below comparable foundation compensation while reflecting the substantial growth in organizational impact and responsibility.

GRANTING UNRESTRICTED FUNDS ABOVE 20% THRESHOLD

The board approved a proposal to grant to recommended charities the amount in excess of 20% of an unrestricted contribution from a particular donor. This policy addressed concerns about dependence on any single donor and ensured diverse funding base for operations while directing large unrestricted gifts primarily to recommended charities.

This affected one large unrestricted gift, requiring approximately $250,000 to be granted out to top charities. The policy provided a clear framework for handling major unrestricted donations going forward, balancing the need for operational funding with the principle of limiting concentration risk and staying true to GiveWell's core mission of directing funds to outstanding giving opportunities.

EXPENSE APPROVAL THRESHOLD

The board approved amending Article 6, Section 2 of the bylaws to provide more flexibility for the Executive Director to incur expenditures in the ordinary course of business. The previous requirement for board approval of expenditures exceeding $25,000 was replaced with requiring board approval for unbudgeted expenses in amounts up to 10% of the previously board-approved 12-month expenses for GiveWell.

This streamlined decision-making while maintaining appropriate oversight. With annual budgets of several million dollars, requiring full board approval for every $25,000+ expense created administrative burden for routine operational decisions. The new 10% threshold (approximately $640,000 based on $6.4 million annual budget) provided Executive Director flexibility for normal operations while ensuring board review of truly significant unbudgeted expenditures. Expenses exceeding this threshold would require approval from the Audit Subcommittee rather than the full board, further professionalizing financial governance.

PROGRESS ON OPEN PHILANTHROPY PROJECT SEPARATION

The board engaged in extensive open-ended discussion of topics including update on progress splitting GiveWell and Open Philanthropy into separate legal entities. This discussion built on the decision from earlier in the year to pursue formal separation by end of 2016.

The separation involved complex legal, financial, operational, and strategic considerations. Key issues included: valuation of Open Philanthropy Project assets being transferred; ownership of intellectual property, trademarks, and web properties; allocation of contracts, employees, and liabilities; cost-sharing arrangements for shared services and office space; and governance structures for the new entity.

FORMALIZING BOARD RESPONSIBILITIES AND PROFESSIONALIZATION

Discussion topics included a suggestion to formalize board members' responsibilities and professionalize the board. As GiveWell grew from startup to established nonprofit with complex operations, more formal governance structures became appropriate.

Board professionalization likely addressed: clearer definition of board member roles and time commitments; establishment of committees with defined responsibilities (audit, strategy, compensation); formal processes for board evaluations and development; succession planning for board leadership positions; and documentation of board policies and procedures.

The formation of the Open Philanthropy Split Subcommittee (mentioned in later meetings) represented one example of this professionalization, creating a specialized group to handle the complex separation process with appropriate independence and expertise.

GROWTH IN MONEY MOVED AND OUTREACH

Discussion covered an update on GiveWell's growth in money moved and outreach efforts. Money moved continued strong growth trajectory, with combined GiveWell and Open Philanthropy Project moving approximately $117 million in 2015 compared to approximately $36 million in 2014.

This dramatic growth reflected both GiveWell's expanding influence on individual donors and small foundations, and the Open Philanthropy Project's rapidly scaling grantmaking in partnership with Good Ventures. The growth validated the research quality and demonstrated substantial impact justifying continued organizational investment and expansion.

FUTURE ORGANIZATIONAL GROWTH PLANS

Discussion addressed GiveWell's plans for future organizational growth, specifically what GiveWell's recent history of new top charities implied about expectations for future staff size and type of staff needed. This strategic question linked research output to organizational capacity planning.

Analysis of how many new top charities GiveWell was identifying annually, how much investigation capacity was required per potential charity, and what kinds of expertise were needed (generalist researchers, specialists in specific interventions or geographies, M&E experts, etc.) would inform hiring plans and budget projections. The discussion helped align organizational development with strategic research priorities.

STAFF COMPENSATION CHANGES

Discussion covered changes to staff compensation beyond the co-Executive Directors. As GiveWell professionalized and competed for top research talent in expensive San Francisco, competitive compensation for all levels became increasingly important for recruitment and retention.

INSURANCE AND ADMINISTRATIVE MATTERS

The board reviewed required annual items including document retention and destruction policy (unchanged from previous year) and insurance coverage. A notable addition since the last review was coverage for employee dishonesty (theft/embezzlement) and hired and non-owned auto, representing evolution in risk management as the organization grew. Workers' Compensation and Employers' Liability coverage was now provided through Hartford in addition to the Philadelphia Insurance Companies coverage for other policies.

The board discussed but did not have coverage for earthquake insurance, agreeing to investigate purchasing it during the next annual renewal review. Operating in San Francisco, earthquake risk represented a potential business continuity concern worth evaluating more thoroughly.

CHECK-IN ON GIVEWELL/GOOD VENTURES RELATIONSHIP

As in previous meetings, the board checked in on the GiveWell/Good Ventures relationship. Things continued to go well, with the discussion structured to allow questions, including having Cari Tuna step out to allow board members to discuss any concerns in her absence if desired.

The relationship had evolved substantially, with Good Ventures providing majority funding for Open Philanthropy Project, significant operating support for traditional GiveWell work, and in-kind office space. The pending separation of Open Philanthropy Project would clarify and formalize many aspects of this relationship while maintaining the productive partnership that had developed since 2011-2012.
