GIVEWELL BOARD MEETING #35 - MARCH 27, 2017

OPEN PHILANTHROPY PROJECT SPLIT - FORMAL APPROVAL

This meeting represented the culmination of months of preparation for the formal separation of the Open Philanthropy Project from GiveWell. The board engaged in extensive deliberation and voted to approve the transfer of Open Philanthropy Project assets to the Open Philanthropy Project LLC.

Timothy Ogden, Chair of the Open Philanthropy Project Split Subcommittee, updated the board on the status of the transfer and reviewed the Subcommittee's thorough process. The Subcommittee had hired an independent accounting firm to complete a valuation of assets being transferred and engaged Morgan, Lewis & Bockius LLP to provide legal advice on various aspects of the transaction.

Celia Roady from Morgan Lewis explained legal considerations associated with the transfer, including relevant requirements under federal tax laws and state nonprofit corporation law. The transaction had to satisfy requirements for tax-exempt organizations selling assets, ensuring fair market value compensation and compliance with regulations preventing private benefit or excess benefit transactions.

Board members with conflicts disclosed their interests. Holden Karnofsky would become a full-time employee of the LLC. Cari Tuna and Dustin Moskovitz expected to be the funders of the LLC. Elie Hassenfeld would bill approximately 25% of his time to the LLC initially while remaining employed by GiveWell. Cari, Holden, and Elie all anticipated serving on the LLC's board (though not yet finalized). These conflicted parties confirmed they would leave the meeting for board deliberation.

Elie discussed the anticipated impact of the transfer on GiveWell. The separation would enable GiveWell to focus on its core mission and draw clearer distinctions between GiveWell and Open Philanthropy activities through separate brands, staff, and fundraising. Based on donor conversations, the transfer should have no material impact on GiveWell's fundraising beyond removal of Good Ventures support for the Open Philanthropy Project itself. While some GiveWell donors were more interested in supporting Open Philanthropy activities, this was the exception rather than the rule.

The board discussed alternatives to the transfer, noting that the Project was not income-producing and other purchasers were unlikely. Board members acknowledged that Open Philanthropy employees could always leave GiveWell and work for the LLC regardless of asset transfer terms. The board discussed the cost-sharing agreement that would enable GiveWell and the LLC to share services of certain employees (operations, reception) and certain expenses on a cost-reimbursement basis.

Board members confirmed the LLC intended to compensate employees similarly to GiveWell despite not being subject to Section 501(c)(3) restrictions. The LLC did not expect to earn profit from commercial activities or investments and did not intend for employees to receive equity or profit-sharing. Activities would continue much as conducted previously, but the Project would be defined under its own terms rather than as a GiveWell-Good Ventures collaboration.

The Subcommittee walked through the negotiation process and recent term sheet changes. The independent valuation report presented a range for fair market value of assets being transferred. The board considered and would rely on a valuation within this range when setting the price. After conflicted parties left the room, the board engaged in further deliberation about the term sheet, proposed transfer, and valuation process, affirming that this unique transaction allowed reliance on the full range of fair market valuations presented.

The unconflicted board members unanimously approved resolutions authorizing the process for negotiating final terms for the acquisition, with Timothy Ogden and Rob Reich designated to complete negotiations taking the valuation into account, subject to final board approval. The board also authorized officers to enter into a license agreement for continued office space use and a cost-sharing agreement, provided GiveWell would pay no more than fair market value for LLC services and the LLC would pay no less than fair market value for GiveWell services.

MODIFICATION TO EXCESS ASSETS POLICY

The board unanimously approved amending the excess assets policy to exclude revenue from the Open Philanthropy Project sale from the policy until one year after the transaction. The policy normally required GiveWell to irrevocably earmark excess unrestricted assets for recommended charities at each budget meeting.

Given the anticipated closing in May and budget meeting in June, the board needed more time to evaluate future activities before applying the excess assets policy to sale proceeds. The one-time nature of this transaction justified special treatment, allowing time to develop a thoughtful budget against which to assess whether proceeds constituted excess assets.

CHANGING THRESHOLD FOR BOARD APPROVAL OF EXPENSES

The board unanimously approved modifying the bylaws threshold for board approval of expenses. The current requirement of board approval for expenses exceeding $25,000 created logistical challenges given organizational growth. The new approach required Audit Committee approval for unbudgeted expenses exceeding 3% of GiveWell's previously board-approved budget.

For 2017's unique circumstances with the possible spinout, the threshold was set at expenses exceeding $60,000 as an approximation until the June budget meeting. The board also approved up to $100,000 for purchase of advertisements and podcasts through Vox, representing investment in outreach to grow GiveWell's audience and influence.

RATIFICATION OF AUDIT AND STRATEGY SUBCOMMITTEES

The board unanimously ratified the creation of the Audit Subcommittee and Strategy Subcommittee that had been formed during summer 2016, and ratified the appointment of members to these subcommittees. The Audit Subcommittee consisted of Brigid Slipka, Timothy Ogden, and Tom Rutledge (Chair). The Strategy Subcommittee consisted of Julia Wise, Timothy Ogden, and Tom Rutledge, with Holden and Cari abstaining from this vote due to conflicts.

These subcommittees represented formalization of board governance as GiveWell matured. The Audit Subcommittee would oversee financial reporting, internal controls, and compliance. The Strategy Subcommittee would support long-term strategic planning and major organizational decisions. Creating these structures enabled more thorough work on complex issues while maintaining efficient full board meetings.

DOCUMENT DESTRUCTION

The board approved destruction of Holden's box of documents from pre-2012 that existed only in paper form. This routine administrative matter reflected implementation of the document retention and destruction policy, ensuring appropriate record-keeping while not maintaining unnecessary archives.

SIGNIFICANCE OF THIS MEETING

This meeting represented a watershed moment in GiveWell's history. The formal approval of the Open Philanthropy Project separation, after months of careful preparation and deliberation, enabled both organizations to focus on their distinct missions while maintaining appropriate relationships. The professionalization of board governance through subcommittees and refined expense approval processes reflected organizational maturation. GiveWell was transitioning from a small startup that had organically expanded into multiple areas to a focused, professionally governed nonprofit with clear mission and methods.
