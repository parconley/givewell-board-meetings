Clarity on this issue was essential for effective planning and operations for both GiveWell and the newly independent Open Philanthropy Project.

GIVEWELL STRATEGY AND RESEARCH AGENDA

The board received a comprehensive 12-minute update on GiveWell's strategy and research agenda. This represented the longest discussion item of the meeting and covered the most critical strategic questions for GiveWell's future as an independent entity following the Open Philanthropy split.

The strategy update outlined five key principles for GiveWell's future impact. First, the primary ways GiveWell would have significant impact were by recommending the best giving opportunities in global health and development and attracting as much funding as possible to those recommendations. This reaffirmed GiveWell's core mission while acknowledging that both research quality and outreach were essential for impact.

Second, it was crucial that GiveWell identify and recommend new giving opportunities. Without new recommendations, GiveWell would eventually only recommend GiveDirectly, because room for more funding for deworming and bednets would likely eventually be exhausted. This would cause loss of existing donors and difficulty attracting new donors. The strategic imperative was clear: GiveWell must continuously identify new outstanding giving opportunities to maintain donor confidence and organizational relevance.

Third, GiveWell intended to find new top charities via three approaches: (a) work consistent with historical methods (investigating charities implementing proven cost-effective interventions in global health); (b) additional specialized hires allowing consideration of new areas within global health and development such as education and agriculture; and (c) recommending grants to Good Ventures, Open Philanthropy, or others to support development of future GiveWell top charities through incubation grants.

Fourth, with new recommendations, GiveWell would have impact by retaining existing donors, continuing to attract new donors, and directing funding to higher impact opportunities than otherwise available. This articulated a clear theory of change linking research to donor engagement to charitable impact.

Fifth, operational priorities included increasing focus on strengthening operations for efficient, effective work without distraction, with particular attention to risk management, donations processing technology, and back-office operations. The Open Philanthropy/GiveWell split would not meaningfully affect GiveWell's funding situation, as GiveWell was in a stable financial position supported by diverse funders and would maintain current budgeting and fundraising approaches.

This strategy represented a mature, focused vision for GiveWell as an independent entity: maintain excellence in global health and development charity evaluation, continuously expand the set of outstanding giving opportunities, build the organizational and operational infrastructure to support quality research and effective outreach, and attract growing funding to recommended charities. The strategy acknowledged both continuity with GiveWell's founding mission and evolution in methods and scope.

OTHER TOPICS: EXPLAINING THE SPLIT TO THE PUBLIC

The agenda noted discussion of explaining the split to the public. This communications question was important for maintaining donor confidence and clarity about what each organization did. GiveWell needed to explain why the separation made sense, what would change and what would stay the same, and how donors supporting GiveWell's traditional work would benefit from the increased organizational focus.

The challenge was communicating complex organizational restructuring in ways that different stakeholder groups would understand and find reassuring. Long-time GiveWell donors needed assurance that research quality and focus on finding outstanding global health charities would continue. Open Philanthropy Project supporters needed clarity about the new independent entity. Prospective donors needed simple, clear messaging about what GiveWell did and recommended.
