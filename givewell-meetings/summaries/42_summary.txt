Episode 42: Board Meeting - August 25, 2020

Meeting Details:
- Date: August 25, 2020
- Duration: 1 hour 10 minutes
- Format: Video conference (COVID-19 pandemic)
- Chair: Tim Ogden

Summary:

This summer 2020 board meeting, conducted entirely via video conference due to the ongoing pandemic, covered the full spectrum of annual administrative reviews, financial planning, executive compensation, and strategic initiatives. The meeting demonstrated GiveWell's continued operational excellence despite remote work challenges and positioned the organization for international expansion.

Board Leadership and Meeting Format Evolution:

Tim Ogden opened the meeting by introducing two meta-level issues for board consideration:

Revising Meeting Format:
With more substantive discussions happening online prior to meetings through shared documents and asynchronous communication, Tim suggested reconsidering the meeting format to better reflect this shift. Rather than using limited synchronous meeting time to cover material board members had already reviewed, meetings could focus on open questions, strategic decisions, and areas requiring real-time discussion.

Succession Planning:
Tim initiated discussion about developing an approach to succession planning—both for executive leadership and potentially for board positions. This forward-looking governance consideration reflected organizational maturity and appropriate risk management.

These topics demonstrated thoughtful board leadership focused on continuously improving governance effectiveness rather than simply maintaining established patterns.

Comprehensive Annual Administrative Review:

Whitney Shinkle led the board through extensive annual administrative due diligence, with most content provided in advance for review:

Conflict of Interest: All directors and officers had submitted signed conflict of interest statements affirming they had received, read, understood, and agreed to comply with the policy.

Compensation and Benefits: The board certified that compensation arrangements and benefits were reasonable, based on competent survey information, and resulted from arm's-length bargaining—critical representations for maintaining tax-exempt status.

Roster of Officers and Directors:
Current board composition included:
- Tim Ogden (Chair)
- Holden Karnofsky (Vice Chair)
- Cari Tuna (Director)
- Elie Hassenfeld (Director)
- Julia Wise (Director)

Board advisors included Norma Altshuler (prospective board member), Whitney Shinkle (Director of Operations), and Buddy Shah (Managing Director).

Officers:
- Elie Hassenfeld (President)
- Natalie Crispin (Vice President and Treasurer)
- Whitney Shinkle (Secretary and Vice Treasurer)

Mission and Vision: The board reaffirmed GiveWell's core mission ("We find outstanding giving opportunities and publish the full details of our analysis to help donors decide where to give") and vision ("A world in which donors reward effectiveness in improving lives").

Document Retention Policy: The comprehensive policy was reviewed and found adequate, though noted as not recently updated due to higher competing priorities.

Insurance Coverage: Extensive review of all insurance policies including Directors & Officers Liability ($4M), Employment Practices, General Liability ($2M), Professional Liability, Property coverage, Umbrella Liability ($3M), ERISA ($1M), Employee Theft/Fraud ($1M), and Workers Compensation. The review noted GiveWell was investigating cyber insurance given increasing data privacy concerns and remote work.

GiveWell's Recent Progress and Future Plans:

The board reviewed a comprehensive document (regularly updated for ongoing board engagement) outlining progress across all strategic domains.

Research Progress:
- Hired two senior researchers in late 2019, plus two additional senior researchers in 2020
- Hired three entry-level researchers
- Continued work on finding more cost-effective giving opportunities through both increased scale at similar cost-effectiveness and broader scope at potentially greater cost-effectiveness
- Individual researchers actively working to identify new giving opportunities

Outreach Achievements:
- Hired Director of Marketing (2019) and built marketing team
- Major gifts program raised over $1 million in counterfactual donations (2019)
- Built comprehensive donor retention program serving donors at three levels (major, mid-level, mass market)
- Systematic donor research program informing strategy and messaging

Institutional Influence:
- Buddy Shah started as Managing Director in July 2020 to lead this emerging work
- Early-stage exploration of influencing donor governments, large foundations, and multilateral institutions
- Goals being refined as the team developed expertise and relationships

Organizational Evolution:
The document noted that GiveWell's nearly 40-person team was "more capable than it has ever been" with progress in every area. Operations had added capacity, top charities work was running smoothly with minimal CEO involvement, and individual researchers were making progress on finding new opportunities.

Financial Planning and Budget Approval:

Whitney presented GiveWell's financial position and proposed budgets:

Budget Proposals:
- Updated 2020 budget (increase from previously approved)
- Proposed 2021 budget of $11,174,579

The substantial budgets reflected continued organizational growth across research, outreach, and operations to support ambitious money moved goals and expanded research scope.

Asset Restriction - Revenue Diversification:
Consistent with GiveWell's policy of limiting any single revenue source to 20% of two years' operating expenses, the board voted to restrict $3,853,472 in unrestricted assets as restricted to grants at GiveWell's discretion. This maintained appropriate revenue diversification while accommodating a substantial individual donation.

International Expansion Plans:

An important strategic initiative was GiveWell's plan to register in the Netherlands and the United Kingdom. This international expansion would:
- Enable more efficient fundraising from donors in these jurisdictions
- Potentially provide tax benefits to international donors
- Expand GiveWell's operational presence beyond the United States
- Support long-term growth in money moved globally

The board unanimously approved proceeding with these registrations, representing a significant milestone in GiveWell's evolution from a U.S.-focused organization to a truly international operation.

Staff Compensation Protocol:

The board approved a formal compensation protocol establishing systematic procedures for setting and reviewing staff compensation. This protocol would ensure:
- Consistent, fair, and transparent compensation decisions
- Competitive compensation to attract and retain top talent
- Appropriate benchmarking against comparable organizations
- Regular review cycles aligned with organizational planning

The formalization of compensation procedures reflected organizational maturity and commitment to professional HR practices.

Executive Compensation Review:

Following presentation of benchmarking data and performance assessment, Elie, Whitney, and Buddy recused themselves while Tim led an executive session on CEO compensation.

The board unanimously approved the proposed CEO compensation, demonstrating confidence in Elie's leadership and recognition of the organization's strong performance under his direction.

The compensation review process—comprehensive benchmarking, performance assessment, executive session deliberation—followed best practices for nonprofit executive compensation governance.

Governance Actions:

All resolutions were approved unanimously:
1. CEO compensation proposal
2. Updated 2020 and proposed 2021 budgets
3. Restriction of unrestricted funds to regranting
4. Plan to register in Netherlands and UK
5. Staff compensation protocol
6. Minutes from May 28, 2020 meeting
7. Omnibus resolution authorizing officers to execute necessary documents

The unanimous votes reflected board alignment on strategic direction and confidence in management's execution.

COVID-19 Adaptation:

While not a major agenda focus, the continued remote meeting format and references to adapted operations demonstrated GiveWell's successful navigation of pandemic challenges. The organization had:
- Maintained full operations remotely
- Continued pursuing strategic initiatives without major delays
- Sustained staff effectiveness and morale
- Proceeded with planned hiring and expansion

This resilience reflected the strong operational foundation built in previous years and the organization's adaptability.

Looking Forward:

The meeting concluded with GiveWell positioned for continued growth and impact:
- Expanded team with strong capabilities across all functions
- Clear strategic priorities with defined metrics and milestones
- International expansion enabling global fundraising growth
- Systematic planning and governance processes
- Strong financial position supporting ambitious goals
- Professional operations despite remote work challenges

The tone was one of confidence and momentum. Despite pandemic disruption, GiveWell was executing effectively on its mission to find outstanding giving opportunities and direct substantial funding to cost-effective interventions improving lives in global poverty.

The meeting demonstrated that GiveWell had fully matured from a scrappy startup to a sophisticated, professionally managed organization capable of scaling its impact while maintaining the analytical rigor, transparency, and commitment to effectiveness that defined its approach to philanthropy.
