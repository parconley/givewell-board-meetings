An independent narrative of events, constructed by the online community

What follows is Holden’s narrative of events.



Prior to 12/30/2007, both Elie and I posted numerous comments on relevant
websites, promoting the ideas of GiveWell, questioning others’ charity
recommendations, and linking to GiveWell.net .

In no instance did either of us claim that a particular charity was
ineffective, though in many cases we questioned how informed others’
recommendations were (example: “Why DonorsChoose? Why not one of a trillion
other charities? Is there any evidence that improving school supplies
improves education? Is this a better idea than improving the teachers
themselves (Teach for America) or reworking the schools (KIPP, Achievement
First)?”)

We also criticized Charity Navigator’s methodology in many cases.

In all of these instances, we used handles that were variations on Holden
or Elie, and when possible linked our names directly to www.givewell.net .
We were making no attempts to hide our identities, and expected anyone who
followed our comments to become aware of them.  However, we did not
disclose our backgrounds and affiliations in our comments.  We were
mistaken not to do this.

Elie sent one email to the Boing Boing blog that promoted GiveWell without
identifying his role in the project (although it did use his real name).



On 12/30/2007 and 12/31/2007, I carried out three more deceptive forms of
advertising:

    • I sent ten emails to bloggers using an email account I had recently
      created for Teel Lidow, claiming that www.givewell.net had recently
      helped me to make a year-end donation and recommending that they (the
      bloggers) post about it.

    • I made two comments on the Lifehacker blog under the handle Geremiah.
      One asked for guidance in finding a charity; the other, in response to
      a post on Charity Navigator, criticized Charity Navigator and pointed
      to GiveWell.

    • I posted a question to Ask Metafilter under the name Geremiah, asking
      for help finding a good charity for a year-end donation.  I then
      signed out and created a new account, Holden0, and responded under
      this account, recommending GiveWell (without explicitly identifying
      myself as the founder).  Finally, I signed back in as Geremiah, marked
      the Holden0 response as “best answer,” and responded critically to a
      comment by Miko suggesting that “Geremiah” ask a more specific
      question.

My intent was to generate attention and traffic for GiveWell.  I did not
intend for anyone to make a financial or other commitment based on my
falsely represented comments; I intended for these comments to be
intriguing enough to make people investigate GiveWell.net for themselves
and judge it on the merits.  I created obfuscation to promote interest; I
underestimated the extent to which many associate anonymity with
objectivity.

All of this behavior was wrong.  I was overzealous in my enthusiasm to
promote GiveWell, and violated the principle of honesty, which is very
important to me.  I deeply regret this behavior and I am committed to never
again promoting a project of mine without identifying my connection to it
up front.



On 12/31/2007, MetaFilter member Miko uncovered my actions on Ask
Metafilter, and opened a MetaTalk thread to discuss them.  Having had my
two MetaFilter accounts deleted, I created a third (holden00), identified
myself, apologized for my actions, and asked for suggestions on how I could
make up for my breach of the community rules.  I offered a donation,
stating, “I'm not offering this in return for your ceasing your criticism,
I'm offering it to make up for abusing the rules,” and retracted the offer
immediately upon comments that it was perceived as a “bribe” offer.

On a MetaFilter member’s suggestion, I posted an apology to the GiveWell
blog.  The apology initially was only for the misbehavior on MetaFilter.
After a poster requested that I disclose all behavior that falls under the
heading of “astroturfing,” I edited my apology to disclose all of the other
activity mentioned above: my emails to bloggers, my comments on the
Lifehacker blog, and my earlier comments that identified me to interested
parties but did not put my affiliation up front.



Aside from the behavior on Ask MetaFilter, every instance of astroturfing
that has been found by the MetaFilter community was brought up by me before
anyone else found it (in category and general nature; my apology did not
list specific locations and quotes).[1]



This is a complete account of my relevant behavior.

                        Clear Fund Board of Directors
                             Official Statement
                        (Outcome of 1/3/2008 Meeting)
The Board of Directors wishes to emphasize that GiveWell was, is,  and  will
remain committed to the goal of helping those  in  need  by  performing  and
publishing high quality research on charities within  selected  spheres  for
free and discretionary use by all interested parties. We  are  committed  to
the goals of improving  philanthropy  and  increasing  transparency  in  the
sector by publicly sharing our process,  our  criteria,  our  methodologies,
our deliberations, and our findings. Furthermore,  we  remain  committed  to
engaging in public dialogue about  how  best  to  achieve  these  goals  and
encourage continued comment about our organization’s work.
The Board believes that the acts of misrepresentation  that  were  committed
are  indefensible  and  are  in  direct  conflict  with  the  goals  of  the
organization, and we condemn them in the strongest possible terms.
The board apologizes for any violations of public trust resulting  from  Mr.
Karnofsky’s actions.
Effective January 3, 2008,  Holden  Karnofsky  has  been  removed  from  his
position as Executive Director of GiveWell and from his  position  as  Board
Secretary. The Executive Director position is now  vacant;  Tim  Ogden  will
serve as interim Board Secretary. In addition  to  being  removed  from  his
positions, a financial penalty has been imposed on Mr. Karnofsky.  While  we
are removing him from the Executive Director position, we believe  that  his
previous contributions outside of the acts noted above have  demonstrated  a
commitment to the goals of the  organization  and  have  been  important  to
accomplishing GiveWell’s work. As a  result,  Holden  will  be  moved  to  a
Program Officer position, where we believe he will be  valuable  in  helping
GiveWell meet its outstanding commitments to applicants and donors. He  will
also participate in a program of professional development and mentoring.
In response to the actions by Elie Hassenfeld which were  inconsistent  with
GiveWell’s core values of transparency and honesty, the  board  has  decided
to impose a financial penalty. We believe  that  Mr.  Hassenfeld’s  previous
commitment to the goals of GiveWell demonstrates that  he  can  continue  to
make a positive contribution to the work of the organization in  the  future
in his current role as a program officer.
The GiveWell Board of Directors will begin meeting bi-weekly to develop  and
approve operating plans for  GiveWell  and  to  discuss  the  organization’s
strategy and future. We will institute an  organization-wide  communications
policy that will mandate  that  all  staff  and  members  of  the  Board  of
Directors explicitly disclose their affiliation with the  organization  when
communicating with others. The policy will be made public once it  has  been
completed.
We would also like to make clear to all donors to GiveWell since December  1
that we will contact them directly  about  these  recent  changes  and  will
provide them the opportunity to have their gift returned if they so  choose.

Signed,
Bob Elliott, Chairman
Greg Jensen, Treasurer
Tim Ogden, Interim Secretary
Lucy Bernholz
Virginia Ford
