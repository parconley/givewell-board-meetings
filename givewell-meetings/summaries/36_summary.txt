Episode 36: Board Meeting - March 22, 2018

Meeting Details:
- Date: March 22, 2018
- Duration: 1 hour 8 minutes
- Format: Conference call
- Chair: Tom Rutledge

Summary:

This annual spring board meeting focused on reviewing organizational progress from 2017 and establishing plans for 2018, while also addressing important administrative and governance matters to support GiveWell's continued growth and professionalization.

2017 Progress and 2018 Plans:

Elie Hassenfeld led a comprehensive discussion on GiveWell's achievements in 2017 and strategic priorities for 2018. Key topics included significant progress in building out the leadership team with two critical hires that would prove transformative for the organization.

The board learned that GiveWell had successfully recruited a Director of Operations and was in the process of hiring a Head of Growth—both positions representing major investments in organizational capacity. These hires reflected GiveWell's maturation from a primarily research-focused organization to one building comprehensive capabilities across research, operations, and outreach.

The presentation covered GiveWell's plans for substantially increased outreach efforts in 2018, representing a strategic shift toward more proactive engagement with current and potential donors. This aligned with the organization's goal to significantly increase money moved to top charities over the coming years.

Progress on charity and intervention research was reviewed, with the board receiving updates on new areas of investigation and the deepening of analysis for existing recommendations. The research team continued to strengthen its processes and expand its scope while maintaining the high quality standards that defined GiveWell's work.

Administrative and Governance Actions:

The board approved several important administrative resolutions that would enhance operational flexibility and improve governance structures.

Approval of Prior Meeting Minutes:
The board unanimously approved the minutes from the October 30, 2017 board meeting, following standard practice of documenting and ratifying prior decisions. As part of GiveWell's commitment to transparency, the resolution authorized redaction or modification of minutes as necessary before public posting to protect confidential information while maintaining accountability.

Amendment to Bylaws  - Check and Note Signing Authority:
A significant governance enhancement involved amending the bylaws to clarify and update the Executive Director's authority to delegate check and note signing responsibilities. The previous bylaws had been somewhat ambiguous about delegation, and this amendment explicitly confirmed that:

1. The Executive Director could sign checks, drafts, promissory notes, and other evidence of indebtedness representing unbudgeted expenses up to 10% of the previously board-approved 12-month expenses
2. The Executive Director could delegate this authority to any employee of GiveWell while remaining ultimately responsible for ensuring compliance with all bylaw requirements
3. Expenses exceeding these thresholds required approval from the board's audit committee

This amendment was not creating new authority but rather clarifying existing practice and ensuring the bylaws accurately reflected the board's intent. It provided operational flexibility while maintaining appropriate financial controls and oversight.

Formation of Grant-Making Subcommittee:

One of the most significant governance developments was the creation of a specialized Grant-Making Subcommittee. This represented an important evolution in how GiveWell managed its grantmaking from restricted funds.

Previously, GiveWell had required quarterly full-board votes to authorize charitable grants from funds restricted to supporting specific organizations or restricted to making grants at GiveWell's discretion. As the volume and frequency of grantmaking grew, this process had become cumbersome and potentially delayed time-sensitive  grant decisions.

The new subcommittee structure authorized a smaller group of board members to approve grants more efficiently. The initial subcommittee composition included:
- Holden Karnofsky (Chair)
- Tom Rutledge
- Julia Wise

Key features of the subcommittee structure included:
- Authority to authorize the Executive Director to make charitable grants from restricted funds
- The Executive Director could delegate authority to request subcommittee votes to any GiveWell employee
- Subcommittee members' terms coincided with their terms on the full board
- The structure maintained governance oversight while enabling more nimble grantmaking decisions

This delegation model reflected best practices in nonprofit governance—empowering specialized committees to handle specific domains while the full board retained ultimate oversight responsibility.

Organizational Evolution:

The meeting reflected GiveWell's transition toward more sophisticated organizational structures. The establishment of the Grant-Making Subcommittee, clarification of delegation authority, and focus on hiring senior operational leaders all indicated an organization moving beyond its startup phase.

The board's discussions demonstrated appropriate oversight of strategic direction while avoiding micromanagement of operational decisions. The governance enhancements put in place would enable faster, more flexible decision-making as GiveWell scaled its operations and impact.

Transparency and Accountability:

Throughout the administrative actions, GiveWell maintained its strong commitment to transparency. Resolutions consistently included provisions for public disclosure of meeting minutes and decisions, with appropriate redactions only for confidential donor information or other legitimately private matters.

This transparency extended to the grantmaking process as well. While the Grant-Making Subcommittee would handle routine grant approvals more efficiently, the structure maintained clear documentation and accountability for all grantmaking decisions.

Looking Ahead:

The meeting positioned GiveWell for an ambitious 2018. With leadership additions in operations and growth, enhanced governance structures for more efficient grantmaking, and clear strategic priorities around research and outreach expansion, the organization had the infrastructure to pursue its goals of finding and funding outstanding giving opportunities at increasing scale.

The tone was one of organizational confidence tempered with awareness of the substantial work ahead. GiveWell had built strong foundations and was now investing in the capabilities needed to achieve step-function growth in its impact on global health and poverty alleviation.

The meeting concluded with unanimous approval of all proposed resolutions, reflecting board alignment on the strategic direction and governance enhancements necessary to support GiveWell's continued evolution and expanding impact.
