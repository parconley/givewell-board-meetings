GIVEWELL BOARD MEETING #26 - APRIL 30, 2014

FINANCIAL POSITION AND BUDGET

The board reviewed GiveWell's overall budget situation, which showed current reserves of $2.00 million against 12-month forward expenses of $1.52 million and 12-month forward revenues of $1.95 million. While the current position appeared healthy, projections showed 12-month forward expenses exceeding 12-month revenues beginning in December 2014. The organization was projected to hold less than one year of reserves as of July 2014, making fundraising an important continuing priority.

Revenue sources reasonably likely to recur totaled $1,944,304 across 358 donors. The breakdown by donor size showed: 6 donors giving $100,000+ contributing $1,040,000; 3 donors giving $50,000-$99,000 contributing $150,000; 3 donors giving $20,000-$49,000 contributing $71,000; 20 donors giving $10,000-$19,000 contributing $396,700; 17 donors giving $5,000-$9,000 contributing $98,000; and 310 donors giving under $5,000 contributing $188,604.

The diversity of funding sources showed Good Ventures providing 12% of 12-month forward revenues, the 5 largest donors providing 48%, the 10 largest donors providing 67%, and the 20 largest donors providing 76%. This concentration indicated both opportunity and risk, with a relatively small number of major donors providing the bulk of operating support while a growing base of smaller donors provided diversification and connection to GiveWell's core individual donor audience.

The board provisionally voted to approve the budget, with formal approval scheduled for June. This two-stage process allowed time for refinement based on the April discussion while providing directional guidance for organizational planning.

STAFF EXPANSION AND RECRUITING

GiveWell made substantial progress expanding staff, growing from 5 to 11 full-time staff members in 2013. This expansion enabled the organization to increase the number of organizations it could consider for traditional top charities research and accelerate GiveWell Labs work. For 2014, GiveWell planned to expand entry-level Research Analysts from approximately 4 to at least 6 and possibly more.

The organization planned a summer intern class of 4-6 people, hoping some would become full-time hires post-graduation. This represented a more systematic approach to building the talent pipeline through internships that could convert to permanent positions. GiveWell also hired 5 conversation notes writers who worked remotely with sole responsibility for preparing conversation notes for publication, allowing research staff to focus on core analytical work.

Significantly, GiveWell made its first senior, cause-specific hire for Labs by bringing on Shayna Strom as US Policy Director. This marked a new phase in GiveWell Labs development, moving from exploratory work led by co-founders to building dedicated capacity in specific cause areas. The hire signaled commitment to expanding beyond global health and development into domestic policy.

The organization continued increasing the number of consultants, now working with approximately 10. This flexible workforce model allowed GiveWell to access specialized expertise and scale capacity for specific projects without the long-term commitment of full-time hires.

OUTREACH ACTIVITIES

While research remained the primary focus rather than outreach, GiveWell maintained several outreach initiatives. The organization held semi-annual research events for donors in New York City and San Francisco, adding Boston for the first time in 2014. These events provided opportunities for major donors to engage directly with GiveWell's research and staff.

GiveWell aimed to conduct one-on-one calls with all donors who had given at least $10,000 to build relationships. This personalized approach recognized that major donors represented both a significant portion of funding and potentially the most engaged and influential audience for GiveWell's work.

The organization engaged in proactive outreach in the Bay Area following the San Francisco relocation, holding 3 meetings with potentially major donors. One individual was now supporting GiveWell and intended to support future recommendations. Another appeared reasonably well aligned but had not yet started giving substantially. The final case was less clear. These results suggested the Bay Area strategy was beginning to yield results, though major donor cultivation remained a long-term process requiring sustained relationship building.

METRICS AND GROWTH TRAJECTORY

Metrics as of April 28, 2014 showed continued growth across key indicators. The total number of donors who supported top charities increased substantially from 2012 through 2014, though specific numbers were presented in chart form. Amounts given to top charities showed growth across all donation size categories, with particularly strong growth in larger donation categories.

Web traffic excluding AdWords grew 15% in the first part of 2014 versus 2013 (205,821 visits vs. 177,731). Organic unpaid Google search provided the largest traffic source, followed by CPC (paid Google search), direct traffic, and referral traffic via external web links. The 15% growth rate was healthy but represented deceleration from the very high growth rates of earlier years, consistent with the maturation of the organization and the reality that exponential growth cannot continue indefinitely.

STRATEGIC CONTEXT

This meeting occurred during a period of organizational transition. GiveWell had successfully relocated to San Francisco, substantially expanded staff, and begun making concrete investments in GiveWell Labs with the hiring of a dedicated senior researcher. The organization was executing on its strategy to build capacity while managing the financial challenges of growth.

The provisional budget approval process reflected board engagement in financial oversight while allowing flexibility for refinement. The emphasis on recruiting and staff development showed recognition that organizational capacity remained the primary constraint on achieving ambitious research and influence goals.

The outreach discussion demonstrated a balanced approach: research remained the priority, but targeted relationship building with major donors was receiving increased attention. The Bay Area relocation appeared to be creating new opportunities for donor engagement, though it was early to assess the full impact of the geographic shift.
