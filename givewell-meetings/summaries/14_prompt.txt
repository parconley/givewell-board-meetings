=== Progress Report ===

                   GiveWell Progress Report, Mid-Year 2011

Our priorities for the year, as articulated in our annual report,[1] are:

1. Research expansion: finding more top charities (enough to productively
absorb $3 million).
    • We have struggled on this front despite devoting the vast bulk of our
      resources and attention to it. We discuss our situation in Attachment
      C.
    • This will continue to be our overwhelmingly top priority through
      Thanksgiving of this year.
    • We have begun work on a new research initiative, but are early in the
      process and do not expect this initiative to generate concrete
      recommendations in 2011.

2. Fundraising: raising 2011 revenues to around $400,000.00.
    • We now project 2011 revenues of around $540,000.00 though not all of
      this funding is expected to repeat in 2012.
    • Our reserves policy is not currently triggered, as discussed in
      Attachment A.
    • We have de-prioritized fundraising for the time being.

3. Research maintenance and systemization: integrating new hires into our
process and updating our recommendations.
    • We consider ourselves on track for this goal.
    • We currently have a 7-person team and are happy with the productivity
      and management overhead; two of our employees are temporary (leaving
      at the end of August) and we hope to expand our team more permanently
      in the coming year.
    • We don't yet have a plan for recruiting.

4. Research vetting: securing more testimonials and external reviews.
    • We feel that our testimonials page[2] has improved satisfactorily.
    • Progress has been relatively slow on securing external reviews. We
      plan to raise the priority of this front after Thanksgiving.

5. Marketing: pursuing low-hanging fruit to increase our influence.
    • We have made substantial progress on many of the "low-hanging fruit"
      avenues we listed:
         o Improving search engine optimization and search engine
           marketing: we have enrolled in the Google Grants program,
           substantially increased our advertising, and seen improved
           search engine traffic due largely to optimized blog posts. We
           are in the process of hiring a contractor to help us further in
           this category.
         o Earned media: our work on disaster relief, which we published in
           January, positioned us to make public recommendations for this
           year's crises in Japan and East Africa. In the former case, our
           recommendations received substantial press[3] and in both cases
           we have seen increased web traffic during these periods.
         o Website improvements, including better integration of social
           media (Facebook, Twitter, Google+), a "donation reminder" box
           (though we've seen very little use of this), and an ongoing
           server and CMS upgrade.
         o We have been experimenting with different types of community
           events. We held a fundraising/awareness event targeting our
           former employer on April 28 in Westport, and are holding a
           research-focused community event on August 18 in NYC.
    • Statistics on our impact, updated quarterly, are now available at
      http://www.givewell.org/about/impact .
         o Traffic was up 87% from last year's Q1 to this year's, and 209%
           from last year's Q2 to this year's. These improvements include
           periods of major coverage of the Japan crisis, and we expect
           less traffic in the remainder of 2011.
         o The number of donations through the website is up about 160%
           from the first half of last year to the first half of this year.
           Money moved through the website is up about 300% from the first
           half of last year to the first half of this year. Disaster-
           related donations play very little role in this improvement.
         o We don't have a good handle on why the improvement in money
           moved is so much greater than the improvement in website
           traffic, but this has been consistently the case going back to
           early 2010.

=== Research Update ===

                   GiveWell Research Update - August 2011

                                   Summary

    • GiveWell's top priority for 2011 is finding additional outstanding
      giving opportunities for donors.
    • We intend to publish our updated recommendations and rankings by
      Thanksgiving 2011.
    • During 2011, we've considered over 450 additional organizations and
      contacted more than 40 for an in-depth review.
    • We have not found organizations that we are extremely confident in. At
      this point:
         o We have 10 organizations we consider "contenders" for our top
           ranking.
         o We think that the Against Malaria Foundation is the most likely
           organization to receive our top ranking at the end of the year.
         o We do not believe we will identify new organizations to contact
           before the end of the year; the 40 organizations we are
           currently reviewing in depth likely comprise the full set of
           organizations that could potentially receive our top ranking
           this year.


            Background: focusing on potential top-rated charities


    • We have shifted our long-term goal from (a) creating a menu of top
      charities by cause to (b) identifying the best organizations we can,
      regardless of cause and reducing our focus on cause-specific analysis
      when it is not geared towards finding a charity we could rank #1.[1]
    • At the end of 2010, VillageReach was our #1-ranked organization and it
      had limited room for more funding. We believe that funding is not
      currently a bottleneck to further expansion of VillageReach’s program,
      and it needs approximately $1 million more over the next 3 years to
      fully fund its project. GiveWell directed more than $1 million to
      VillageReach in 2010, and based on VillageReach’s room for more
      funding, we would not want to move that amount to them again this
      year.
    • Our focus this year is on finding more charities that we can recommend
      as strongly as we have VillageReach. For a variety of reasons, we
      believe that VillageReach may have been a unique opportunity for
      donors. It had a combination of:
         o a program with strong evidence of effectiveness focused on
           immunizations, a highly cost-effective, proven intervention
         o which VillageReach needed funding to scale up further
         o but it was struggling to find funding from traditional,
           institutional donors
         o even though, were it to work, it could had high upside by
           potentially altering the way health supplies are distributed
           across the developing world
    • So, while our goal is to find a charity as strong as VillageReach, we
      believe that those opportunities may not arise for individual donors
      every year.


                                 Our process


    • Our process has become considerably less "rigid" this year. We are
      focused on finding a potential #1 charity, i.e., a charity that we are
      excited to promote. We have expanded the set of things we consider
      "signs of a charity worth investigating" and we have become more open-
      ended in our initial conversations with charities. However, we still
      work toward answering the same set of questions: what do they do,
      what's the evidence that it works, how cost-effective is it, and
      what's the room for more funding.
    • In general, we try to cast the net as wide as we can and "scan" (i.e.,
      briefly consider) as many organizations as we can. This year, in
      addition to reconsidering the close-to-400 international charities
      already listed on our site, we considered an additional approximately
      450 charities we found by:
         o Referrals: organizations submitted to us via the form on our
           website or emails. These come from the charities themselves or
           donors interested in the charity.
         o Organiations supported by foundations such as the Gates
           Foundation or Skoll Foundation which have a focus on
           international aid
         o Organizations recommended by academics such as the ones listed
           on the site for Esther Duflo and Abhijit Banerjee’s book Poor
           Economics
    • We narrow the list down using heuristics. In the past we looked for
      organizations that either (a) focused on our “priority programs”
      (i.e., programs with strong evidence of effectiveness) or (b)
      published significant monitoring and evaluation information on their
      websites. This year, we’ve also flagged organizations for the
      following: (Note that these are flags we use and we have not
      necessarily identified an organization in each category.)
         o Having high potential upside. This includes (a) the possibility
           of extremely high cost-effectiveness; (b) organizations that aim
           to use donations today to become self-sustaining such that they
           do not need donations in the future; (c) organizations running
           and evaluating their programs such that if they fail, it will
           create knowledge about whether the type of program they’re
           trying works or not; and (d) organizations aiming to invent new
           technologies
         o Learning organizations. Organizations that can demonstrate that
           they are monitoring their activities and learning from what goes
           well such that they continually improve
         o Low insulation. Organizations that work closely with a
           particular group of people, and appear to be willing and able to
           understand and respond to these people's needs and feedback
           (rather than implementing pre-determined programming).
         o Local healthcare. Organizations set up to provide basic
           healthcare where it is lacking.
         o Orphans and vulnerable children. Organizations focused on
           providing basic services to children with limited resources /
           adult support.
         o Providing basic needs such as water, sanitation, electricity, or
           shelter.
    • Once we’ve flagged organizations, we decide which organizations to
      contact. We do this by ordering the organization by priority (i.e.,
      the likelihood we assign to an organization ultimately being our #1-
      ranked organization).
    • As part of this exercise, staff periodically rank their top 30
      charities. In this exercise, we try to not focus just on GiveWell’s
      predetermined criteria and charities but imagine which 30 charities we
      would give to if we had to. This exercise has helped us identify some
      organizations we weren’t previously prioritizing.
    • As our staff has increased, we’ve been able to contact more
      organizations. We learn a great deal from a single 1-2 hour
      conversation with a charity representative, ideally the CEO.
    • Earlier in the year, we invested a lot of time in each charity we
      contacted, trying to get to a full, in-depth review and investigation
      before moving on. We decided that this approach was causing us to sink
      too much time into each organization and that our time would be better
      spent having initial conversations (from which we learn a lot) with
      more organizations. Now, we stop investigating organizations once we
      believe their chances of becoming our #1 organization are sufficiently
      low, and move onto others that we know less about.


                               Where we stand


   The charities we've  examined  have  mostly  fallen  into  the  following
   categories:
      •  Charities  aiming  to  create  businesses  or  systems  that  will
        eventually become self-sustaining. In most cases we've had concerns
        about these groups' models and have not seen evidence of any  track
        record of reaching sustainability in the past (even for subsets  of
        their activities).
      • Charities focused directly on raising incomes. We haven't seen  any
        such groups with what we consider  to  be  strong  data  indicating
        effectiveness, or groups whose models we  consider  to  be  clearly
        more promising than cash transfers. (We have encountered  one  cash
        transfer-focused charity but it is very  young  and  has  no  track
        record at this time.)
      • Charities focused on research &  development  of  new  technologies
        (health or otherwise). We haven't been able to get a clear sense of
        room for more funding for any of these groups,  but  feel  that  we
        still may in the future.
      •  Charities  focused  on  delivering  basic  healthcare  or   health
        technologies  (insecticide-treated  net  distribution,   deworming,
        etc.) We find these groups to be  most  promising  because  of  the
        relatively strong track record  and  cost-effectiveness  of  health
        aid. However, we haven't found any groups that we consider to  meet
        the gold standard in answering our key questions.

We haven't found any charities that have a truly compelling data-based  case
for impact. We've largely evaluated charities by a combination  of  (a)  how
promising their program area is; (b) their room for more funding, i.e.,  the
extent to which donations seem likely to fund promising activities; (c)  the
extent to which their representatives seem to be asking  critical  questions
and working toward having better evidence regarding effectiveness.
