GIVEWELL BOARD MEETING #32 - MARCH 21, 2016

OPEN PHILANTHROPY PROJECT UPDATE

Holden Karnofsky provided a comprehensive 40-minute update on the Open Philanthropy Project and answered questions about its plan for the next year. This substantial presentation covered the Project's research progress, grantmaking activities, organizational development, and strategic direction as it continued to evolve from exploratory research to a mature philanthropic advisory operation.

The update likely covered key accomplishments including grants made in priority cause areas, development of research methodologies for evaluating harder-to-measure interventions compared to GiveWell's traditional global health work, expansion of staff capacity in specific domains, and evolution of the partnership with Good Ventures. The discussion of next year's plan would have addressed research priorities, anticipated major grant opportunities, hiring needs, and the increasingly clear need to eventually formalize the separation between Open Philanthropy Project and GiveWell's traditional charity evaluation work.

The length and depth of this update reflected the substantial growth of the Open Philanthropy Project. What began as exploratory research on new cause areas had become a significant operation with dedicated staff, sophisticated research processes, and influence over tens of millions of dollars in annual grantmaking through partnership with Good Ventures.

The board engaged in a 6-minute discussion of GiveWell's relationships with other charity evaluators. This represented attention to GiveWell's position in the broader charitable evaluation ecosystem and relationships with organizations potentially serving as partners, competitors, or complementary resources for donors.

Other charity evaluators at this time would have included Charity Navigator (the largest and most established, focused primarily on financial metrics), GuideStar (providing information infrastructure), and emerging evidence-focused evaluators like ImpactMatters. The discussion likely covered: whether and how to collaborate with other evaluators; how to position GiveWell's distinctive approach and value proposition; handling of charities that received different ratings from different evaluators; and opportunities or challenges arising from the growing attention to effectiveness and impact measurement in philanthropy.

The brief duration suggested this was more of a situational update and strategic check-in than a major policy discussion. GiveWell had largely maintained a strategy of focusing on its own research rather than extensive collaboration or competition with other evaluators, but periodic review of this approach made sense as the landscape evolved.

The board spent 3 minutes discussing the structure of and plan for future board meetings. ORGANIZATIONAL CONTEXT

This March 2016 meeting occurred during a period of continued rapid growth and increasing clarity about the future trajectory of both GiveWell's traditional work and the Open Philanthropy Project. Money moved to recommended charities continued increasing, staff had expanded significantly, and the organization had established sophisticated research processes for both traditional charity evaluation and Open Philanthropy's broader cause area investigations.

However, the increasing scale and success of the Open Philanthropy Project was creating organizational complexity. With Open Philanthropy accounting for the majority of expenses and staff time while serving primarily one major partner (Good Ventures) rather than the broader donor base, questions about appropriate organizational structure were becoming more acute. The extensive Open Philanthropy update and other discussions at this meeting were setting the stage for the eventual separation that would formalize over the following year.
