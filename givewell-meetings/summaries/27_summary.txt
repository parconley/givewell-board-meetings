GIVEWELL BOARD MEETING #29 - MARCH 12, 2015

BOARD EXPANSION

The board approved the addition of Jake Gibson to the Clear Fund Board of Directors. Jake's addition brought valuable expertise and perspective to the organization during a period of significant growth and strategic evolution. The board now consisted of eleven members: Tom Rutledge (Chair), Tim Ogden (Vice-President), Cari Tuna (Treasurer), Holden Karnofsky (Secretary), Elie Hassenfeld, Greg Jensen, Rob Reich, Brigid Slipka, Phil Steinmeyer, Julia Wise (to be added at the June meeting), and now Jake Gibson.

The expansion of the board from the original small group reflected GiveWell's maturation as an organization. A larger, more diverse board provided broader perspectives on strategy, stronger governance oversight, deeper networks for fundraising and partnerships, and greater capacity for committee work and specialized expertise.

RETIREMENT PLAN ESTABLISHMENT

The board approved the creation of a 403(b) retirement plan for GiveWell employees. This represented a significant enhancement to employee benefits and competitive positioning for talent recruitment and retention. Prior to this, GiveWell offered salary and health benefits but no retirement benefits, which put the organization at a disadvantage in competing for high-quality research talent.

The 403(b) plan is the nonprofit equivalent of a 401(k) retirement plan available to for-profit companies. Establishing this benefit signaled GiveWell's commitment to providing competitive total compensation packages that would enable long-term employee retention and career development within the organization. As staff grew from a handful of people to more than a dozen, formalizing benefits became increasingly important for organizational sustainability.

The retirement plan likely included employer contributions (either matching or non-elective) in addition to allowing employee salary deferrals. The specific details of employer contribution levels would have been determined based on budget constraints, competitive positioning, and desire to incentivize long-term employee tenure. This benefit complemented the salary increases for co-Executive Directors approved in previous meetings and represented ongoing evolution toward professional nonprofit compensation practices.

While specific details were not captured in the brief meeting minutes, this discussion covered progress in 2014, assessment of organizational performance, and strategic plans for 2015 and beyond.

Organizational capacity topics likely included: staff growth and composition (number of research analysts, senior researchers, operations staff); recruiting successes and challenges; employee retention and satisfaction; professional development and training initiatives; and evolution of organizational culture as the team expanded.

Financial performance review would have covered: actual revenues versus projections for 2014; diversity of funding sources and concentration risks; success of fundraising initiatives targeting individual donors; institutional funding relationships; and expense management and budget adherence.

The forward-looking plan portion would have outlined priorities for 2015 including: research agenda for traditional charity evaluations (any new intervention areas or charity investigations planned); Open Philanthropy Project research priorities and expected grant activity; hiring targets by role and expected timeline; organizational development initiatives (systems, processes, governance); fundraising goals and strategies; and communications and outreach plans.

The 30-minute discussion indicated substantive board engagement with strategic direction rather than perfunctory approval of management proposals. Board members likely asked probing questions about priorities, resource allocation, risks, and contingency plans, providing valuable oversight and counsel to the co-Executive Directors.

ORGANIZATIONAL CONTEXT

This March 2015 meeting occurred during a period of rapid evolution. GiveWell had completed its first full year of operation in San Francisco following the 2012-2013 relocation from New York. The organization had successfully scaled from 5 to approximately 15+ staff members over two years. The Open Philanthropy Project had transitioned from exploratory research to active grantmaking in partnership with Good Ventures.

The retirement plan approval reflected organizational maturation from startup to established nonprofit. Early-stage organizations often cannot afford comprehensive benefits, focusing resources on mission-critical activities. As GiveWell's budget grew past $2 million annually and the organization employed a significant number of career staff, providing retirement benefits became both financially feasible and strategically important for talent management.

The board expansion similarly reflected organizational evolution. A small board of close advisors and supporters works well for early-stage organizations needing flexibility and quick decision-making. As organizations grow, larger boards provide more diverse expertise, stronger governance, better risk management, and deeper networks. Adding Jake Gibson and preparing to add Julia Wise brought new perspectives and capabilities to board deliberations.

The annual review and planning discussion was becoming more sophisticated as the organization accumulated years of operational data and experience. Early annual reviews focused on survival and basic execution. By 2015, GiveWell could analyze trends over multiple years, benchmark against external comparisons, and make more informed strategic choices about resource allocation and priorities.

The relatively brief 37-minute meeting length, despite covering board expansion, retirement benefits, and comprehensive annual review, suggested efficient board processes and strong alignment between management and governance on strategic direction. The board trusted management execution while providing appropriate oversight and approval of significant decisions.
