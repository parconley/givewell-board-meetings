The Clear Fund Board of Directors convened on January 3, 2008, for an emergency meeting to address serious ethical violations committed by organization leadership. This meeting represented a pivotal moment in GiveWell's early history, testing the organization's commitment to its stated values of transparency and honesty while determining whether the organization could survive a significant breach of public trust.

Nature and Scope of the Ethical Violations

The meeting centered on addressing what the board ultimately characterized as "astroturfing" behavior—deceptive online marketing practices that violated fundamental principles of transparency and honesty. Holden Karnofsky provided a comprehensive narrative documenting his misconduct, which fell into two categories of varying severity.

Prior to December 30, 2007, both Holden Karnofsky and Elie Hassenfeld had posted numerous comments on websites promoting GiveWell's ideas, questioning other charity recommendations, and linking to GiveWell.net. While they used handles that were variations of their real names (Holden or Elie) and linked to www.givewell.net when possible, they failed to explicitly disclose their backgrounds and organizational affiliations in these comments. Elie also sent an email to the Boing Boing blog promoting GiveWell without identifying his role, though using his real name. Both acknowledged this inadequate disclosure was a mistake, even though they were not actively attempting to hide their identities.

The more serious violations occurred on December 30-31, 2007, when Holden Karnofsky engaged in three explicitly deceptive advertising practices. First, he sent ten emails to bloggers using a fake email account created for "Teel Lidow," claiming this fictitious person had used GiveWell.net to make year-end donations and recommending bloggers write about the service. Second, he posted two comments on the Lifehacker blog under the pseudonym "Geremiah"—one requesting charity guidance, another criticizing Charity Navigator while pointing to GiveWell. Third, and most egregiously, he posted a question on Ask MetaFilter under the name "Geremiah" asking for charity recommendations, then logged out, created a new account "Holden0," responded to his own question recommending GiveWell without identifying himself as founder, logged back in as "Geremiah," marked his own response as "best answer," and responded critically to another user's suggestion for more specificity.

Karnofsky stated his intent was generating attention and traffic for GiveWell, not inducing financial commitments based on false representations. He admitted creating obfuscation to promote interest while underestimating how strongly people associate anonymity with objectivity. He characterized all this behavior as wrong, acknowledging he violated principles of honesty that were important to him, expressing deep regret, and committing never again to promote his projects without identifying his connection up front.

Discovery and Response

On December 31, 2007, MetaFilter member "Miko" uncovered Karnofsky's Ask MetaFilter deception and opened a MetaTalk discussion thread. After having two MetaFilter accounts deleted, Karnofsky created a third account ("holden00"), identified himself, apologized, and solicited suggestions for making amends. He offered a financial donation to MetaFilter, explicitly stating this was not intended to stop criticism but rather to make up for rule abuse. When commenters perceived this as an attempted bribe, he immediately retracted the offer—an action the board later characterized as an improper attempt to ameliorate the situation.

Following a MetaFilter member's suggestion, Karnofsky posted an apology to the GiveWell blog, initially addressing only the MetaFilter misconduct. After requests for full disclosure of astroturfing activity, he edited the apology to include the blogger emails, Lifehacker comments, and earlier inadequately disclosed comments. Karnofsky noted that aside from the Ask MetaFilter behavior, every instance of astroturfing found by the MetaFilter community was disclosed by him before others discovered it, though he acknowledged some inadequately disclosed "Holden" comments were found by others before his revised disclosure.

Board Deliberations and Decision Framework

The meeting agenda revealed a structured deliberative process. After introductions and reviewing Holden's narrative alongside the independent MetaFilter community account, the board discussed whether to terminate staff and potentially discontinue GiveWell entirely, appropriate disciplinary measures for retained staff, preventative measures against future problems, and broader improvements to organizational governance, structure, metrics, and model.

Critically, the agenda included a closed session excluding all staff, including Holden, allowing the board to make final determinations without presence of the subjects of disciplinary action. This demonstrated adherence to proper governance protocols for personnel matters involving potential conflicts of interest.

Personnel Decisions and Disciplinary Actions

The board issued a formal official statement announcing major organizational changes effective January 3, 2008. Holden Karnofsky was removed from his position as Executive Director of GiveWell and from his position as Board Secretary, with the Executive Director position remaining vacant and Tim Ogden assuming the role of interim Board Secretary. Beyond removal from leadership positions, a financial penalty was imposed on Karnofsky. However, acknowledging his previous contributions and commitment to organizational goals, the board moved him to a Program Officer position where he could help GiveWell meet outstanding commitments to applicants and donors. He was also required to participate in a program of professional development and mentoring.

Elie Hassenfeld, whose misconduct was characterized as less severe, received a financial penalty but retained his position as Program Officer. The board determined his previous commitment to GiveWell's goals demonstrated capacity for continued positive contribution.

Organizational Reforms and Governance Changes

The board announced several significant structural reforms. Board meetings would begin occurring bi-weekly to develop and approve operating plans and discuss organizational strategy and future—a substantial increase in oversight from previous quarterly or less frequent meetings. An organization-wide communications policy would be instituted mandating that all staff and Board members explicitly disclose their GiveWell affiliation when communicating publicly. This policy would be made public upon completion, demonstrating commitment to the transparency the organization advocated.

In an extraordinary gesture acknowledging potential donor concerns about organizational integrity, the board committed to contacting all donors who had contributed since December 1, offering them the opportunity to have gifts returned if they chose. This represented significant financial risk but demonstrated prioritization of donor trust over immediate organizational funding needs.

Board Assessment and Path Forward

The official statement, signed by Chairman Bob Elliott, Treasurer Greg Jensen, Interim Secretary Tim Ogden, Lucy Bernholz, and Virginia Ford, condemned the misrepresentation acts as "indefensible" and "in direct conflict with the goals of the organization," apologizing for violations of public trust. The board emphasized that GiveWell was, is, and would remain committed to helping those in need through high-quality charity research, improving philanthropy, and increasing sector transparency.

In a separate addendum, Chairman Bob Elliott and Treasurer Greg Jensen, citing their greater personal experience with Holden, provided additional context. While unequivocally stating his identity-concealment actions and donation-offer response were "improper and indefensible," they emphasized their belief in his genuine commitment to GiveWell's cause, noting his substantial financial and personal sacrifices. They defended him against suggestions his involvement was a scam, affirming their observation of his commitment to openness and honesty in previous professional and personal interactions. They characterized his online communication failures as resulting from improper thinking about self-representation rather than fundamentally dishonest character, expressing confidence he had learned from the failure and would demonstrate commitment to transparency values through continued work.

This crisis meeting ultimately determined that GiveWell would continue operating with reformed governance, enhanced board oversight, explicit communication policies, and a leadership team that had been held accountable for serious ethical violations while being given opportunity for redemption through continued service under heightened scrutiny.
