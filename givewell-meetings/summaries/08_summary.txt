GiveWell Board Meeting Summary - Episode 8
Meeting Number: 10
Date: July 27, 2009
Duration: 2 hours 30 minutes (estimated)

OVERVIEW
This board meeting marked a major milestone for GiveWell with the publication of its comprehensive 2008-2009 international aid research report on July 1, 2009, and the approval of its first major grant disbursements totaling over $110,000 to top-rated charities. The meeting also addressed organizational maturation through approval of comprehensive policies and procedures, budget updates, professional development progress, and proposed amendments to organizational bylaws.

PUBLICATION OF MAJOR RESEARCH REPORT
On July 1, 2009, GiveWell published its 2008-2009 recommendations and reasoning for international aid, representing the culmination of over a year of intensive research. This report included reviews of two "top-rated charities" - VillageReach and the Stop Tuberculosis Partnership - that GiveWell had significantly more confidence in than any other charities evaluated to date, including the recommended charities from the 2007-2008 research cycle.

The report also featured reviews of four additional "recommended charities" that GiveWell believed were substantially stronger than other evaluated charities, though not as strong as the top-rated pair. All six recommended or top-rated charities were options for people who had made GiveWell Pledges or GiveWell Advance Donations.

Beyond the charity recommendations, the report included substantial detail on other examined charities and the reasoning behind highlighting specific organizations. This comprehensive approach addressed the breadth-versus-depth tension that had been discussed in previous board meetings.

MAJOR GRANT DISBURSEMENTS APPROVED
The board approved GiveWell's first significant grant disbursements since completing the international aid research:

- Direct grants totaling $85,047.10 to be divided evenly between VillageReach and the Stop Tuberculosis Partnership ($42,523.55 each)
- Recommendations to GiveWell's donor-advised fund to grant an additional $25,047.89, also divided evenly between the two top-rated charities ($12,523.95 each)
- Authority to make other recommendations to the donor-advised fund based on the preferences of donors who had contributed to it

The total disbursements of over $110,000 represented a significant milestone in demonstrating GiveWell's ability to move money to evidence-backed charities. These grants came from the earmarked donations that had been held pending completion of the research and identification of top charities.

The board also reserved the right to ask VillageReach and/or the Stop Tuberculosis Partnership to provide additional information or documentation before disbursing funds, maintaining quality control even after the formal recommendation decision.

FINANCIAL POSITION AND BUDGET REVIEW
The meeting included comprehensive financial reporting covering both historical performance and future projections. Final audited expenses for fiscal year 2008 were reviewed, showing actual spending came in below projections primarily due to the salary penalties imposed on the co-founders for inappropriate marketing ($5,000 reduction each, pro-rated) and lower-than-expected accounting costs.

For the period January 1 through June 30, 2009, final expenses were reviewed. The co-founders' salaries had been restored to $65,000 annually as planned, though some budget line items showed discrepancies due to accounting period adjustments and the inclusion of costs that should have been attributed to 2008.

GiveWell's financial position remained strong. Cash reserves exceeded $600,000, providing substantial runway under various staffing scenarios. Projections showed the organization could operate well into 2010 under reasonable and even aggressive expense assumptions, including potential new hires at different compensation levels.

EXCESS RESERVES POLICY AND PHILOSOPHY
An important philosophical discussion addressed how GiveWell should think about reserve assets. Staff proposed that GiveWell should be an organization that (a) continually has to "earn its existence" and (b) does not excessively hold reserves that could reasonably be granted to accomplish concrete good through outstanding charities.

Consistent with this principle, staff intended to create a formal "Reserve Assets policy" that would define "excess reserves" - reserves beyond what is reasonable to hold for operating purposes that therefore can and should be granted to outstanding charities.

While a formal policy had not yet been settled, for this meeting staff took a fairly conservative approach, defining "excess reserves" as unrestricted reserves over and above what would be needed to operate through June 30, 2010, according to the most conservative projection. This approach balanced organizational sustainability with the commitment to deploying resources for charitable impact rather than accumulating capital.

PRIORITIES FOR REMAINDER OF 2009
Staff outlined major priorities for July 27 through December 31, 2009:

1. Improving and Reality-Checking the 2008-2009 Report: Getting and responding to feedback, researching and awarding a $250,000 grant for economic empowerment in sub-Saharan Africa, adding optional but valuable content to the existing report largely by managing new hires, and visiting the field operations of top charities.

2. Pursuing New Operating Funding Sources: Exploring foundations and fellowships as sources of stable operating funding, with a preliminary list of target foundations and fellowships identified.

3. Annual Self-Evaluation and Planning: Completing a thorough evaluation of the second year and planning for the third year, similar to the comprehensive report completed for the first year.

4. Improving Research Packaging and Presentation: Dramatically improving how research is packaged and presented through both print materials and website improvements, likely involving a professional website consultant.

ECONOMIC EMPOWERMENT GRANT RESEARCH
A specific major priority was researching and awarding a $250,000 grant earmarked for economic empowerment in sub-Saharan Africa, to be disbursed by December 31, 2009. To date, GiveWell had located no outstanding economic empowerment charities despite this being a focus area.

The plan involved compiling a list of potentially eligible charities and inviting them to apply for grants using a process similar to the first-year research process, though with simpler, more specific requests based on independent research during the field-narrowing first round.

Importantly, if GiveWell was unable to identify an outstanding charity, the $250,000 would be used to fund further research and/or evaluation in the economic empowerment area. The donor who provided the earmarked funds had signed off on this contingency plan, demonstrating sophisticated donor understanding that finding truly outstanding opportunities was more important than disbursing funds on a predetermined timeline.

NEW HIRES AND ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY
Since the previous board meeting, GiveWell had hired two new staff members: Melissa Krauss and Natalie Stone. Both were being managed and evaluated, with their work primarily focused on adding "optional but valuable" content to the 2008-2009 report including proofreading, financial analysis of recommended charities, and background on non-recommended interventions.

This represented GiveWell's first successful expansion beyond the two co-founders and demonstrated the organization's increasing ability to leverage additional capacity for systematic research tasks.

PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT PROGRESS
Staff reported substantial progress on the professional development priorities established at the November 10, 2008 meeting. The board had agreed that the co-founders lacked life and business experience and would benefit from mentoring relationships with experienced supporters.

Four mentoring relationships had been developed:

Lindy Miller (Board Member): Weekly phone updates with both co-founders, providing guidance on recruiting, hiring, and managing employees.

Rob Shavell: Periodic in-person and phone updates with a Silicon Valley startup supporter, discussing time management, prioritization, and recruiting/hiring. Updates were less frequent during May and June as staff focused on completing the research report but were expected to increase again with renewed focus on website and packaging improvements. Rob was included on biweekly updates and discussions of name/domain considerations.

Phil Steinmeyer: Periodic phone and email conversations providing big-picture feedback on major challenges and priorities, plus feedback on research progress. Phil was included on biweekly updates and specific discussions.

Adam Goozh: Contacted through the Harvard-Startups email list, offering experience from successfully starting a company. Had general conversations about GiveWell's mission and priorities, provided input on name/domain considerations, and gave a referral for a website consultant.

Regarding formal programs, staff had not pursued entrepreneurship groups since the November 2008 meeting where the board agreed such options held little promise. Investigation of competitive programs (Ashoka, Echoing Green, Draper Richards) revealed that GiveWell was ineligible for Echoing Green (too large) but eligible for Ashoka and Draper Richards. Both accepted rolling applications but had multi-month decision processes. Staff had not yet applied to either.

CO-EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR PROPOSAL
Staff proposed that Holden Karnofsky and Elie Hassenfeld should be formally named co-Executive Directors. The rationale was primarily transparency - both had been filling these roles de facto throughout GiveWell's existence, but the organization officially had no Executive Director.

This created practical problems: certain forms, particularly grant applications, requested the name of the Executive Director, and GiveWell had to respond that it had none. Formalizing the co-Executive Director structure would align the official organizational structure with the operational reality.

COMPREHENSIVE POLICIES AND PROCEDURES MANUAL
The board reviewed and approved a comprehensive Policies and Procedures Manual covering fiscal policies, governance, employment policies, and communications policies. This extensive manual included:

Fiscal Policies: Detailed procedures for revenue recognition and cash receipts, purchasing and accounts payable, credit and debit card use, employee travel and business expenses, approval authorization policies with specific thresholds ($0-$4,999 requiring Independent Executive approval; $5,000-$24,999 requiring Board President and Board Treasurer approval; $25,000+ requiring full Board of Directors approval).

Personnel and Payroll: Procedures for maintaining personnel files, processing payroll, managing independent contractors and consultants, with specific attention to legal compliance including W-4 forms, employee work authorization (I-9 forms), and New York State new hire reporting requirements.

Reporting and Financial Management: Budgeting procedures, monthly close and financial reporting protocols, and a comprehensive document retention policy addressing Sarbanes-Oxley Act requirements.

Assets and Liabilities: Policies for capital assets, investments, insurance, and authorization of debt, including requirements for board resolutions approving debt issuance.

Governance: Finance and audit responsibilities of the Board of Directors, code of ethics, conflict of interest policy, and whistleblower policy addressing Sarbanes-Oxley Act provisions.

Employment Policies: Equal employment opportunity policy, sexual harassment policy, and procedures for resolving discrimination and harassment complaints.

Communications Policies: Protocols for official speaking engagements, mass emails, public internet communication both on and off GiveWell.net, communication with media, and contained communication including emails and personal communication.

Other Policies: Policies on gifts, data backup procedures, and the reserve assets policy framework.

The manual reflected substantial organizational maturation and provided the infrastructure necessary for professional operations as GiveWell grew beyond the two co-founders.

PROPOSED BYLAWS AMENDMENT
Staff proposed amending the bylaws to change language about board meeting frequency. The existing bylaws specified a fixed number of meetings, but in practice the board scheduled meetings at its discretion based on organizational needs. The proposed amendment would replace the specific meeting frequency requirement with "at the Board's discretion" to reflect actual practice.

CORPORATION NAME UPDATE
The board received an update on issues related to the organization's corporate name. The Clear Fund operated publicly as "GiveWell," creating potential confusion and branding complications. Staff were exploring options for aligning the legal corporate name with the public-facing brand, including considerations about domain name availability (the organization used GiveWell.net rather than a .com domain).

FIELD VISITS TO DEVELOPING WORLD
Among the priorities for the remainder of 2009, staff planned to observe and report on the operations of top-rated charities in the field. These visits would involve 1-2 trips total, with each trip costing 1-2 weeks (40-80 working hours lost to regular work). The visits would provide firsthand observation of VillageReach and Stop Tuberculosis Partnership operations, potentially including examination of other representative charities.

This represented an important evolution in GiveWell's research methodology - moving beyond desk research and charity-provided documentation to include direct field observation.

OPTIONAL RESEARCH CONTENT
Staff planned to add substantial optional but valuable content to the existing report, including:

- Paraphrased transcripts of conversations with international aid experts and charity representatives (already in rough form, requiring cleanup and approval from conversation partners)
- More examined charities from submissions through the charity submission form and additional scanned sources
- Writeups on programs commonly run by charities, discussing available information and major concerns (many such writeups already existed, particularly for most promising and most prominent programs)
- Integration of new materials from current recommended charities and updates on "pending" charities

Much of this work would be accomplished by the new hires (Melissa Krauss and Natalie Stone), with staff time estimated at approximately 60 hours for oversight and quality control.

ORGANIZATIONAL TRAJECTORY AND IMPLICATIONS
This meeting demonstrated GiveWell's successful execution of the research-first strategy adopted in late 2008. The organization had delivered on its July 1 commitment to publish comprehensive recommendations, identified genuinely outstanding charities with evidence of effectiveness, secured nearly $350,000 in earmarked donations to regrant, and built the organizational infrastructure necessary for professional operations and growth.

The financial position - over $600,000 in reserves with runway well into 2010 - provided genuine stability. The successful hiring of two new staff members demonstrated increasing organizational capacity. The comprehensive policies manual addressed governance and operational requirements for a maturing nonprofit. The field visit plans showed commitment to deepening research quality beyond desk analysis.

The $250,000 economic empowerment grant research, with its explicit contingency that funds would support further research if no outstanding charity could be identified, demonstrated sophisticated donor alignment and GiveWell's commitment to honest assessment over deployment pressure.

The meeting represented a transition point - from an organization racing to prove its research model could work to one focused on refining and scaling an approach that had demonstrated initial success. The priorities for the remainder of 2009 balanced improving the existing research product, expanding research coverage, professionalizing operations, and building the relationships and infrastructure necessary for sustainable growth.
