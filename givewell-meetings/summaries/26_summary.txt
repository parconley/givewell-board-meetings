The board discussed and ultimately approved the proposed 2015 budget, which reflected continued organizational growth and the increasingly bifurcated nature of GiveWell's work between traditional charity research and the Open Philanthropy Project. The budget incorporated lessons learned from 2014 execution and adjusted for expected staffing levels, research activities, and operational needs.

NEW WEBSITE FOR GIVEWELL AND OPEN PHILANTHROPY PROJECT

A significant agenda item was discussion of plans and budget for new websites for both GiveWell and the Open Philanthropy Project. As the Open Philanthropy Project had matured from exploratory research (previously called GiveWell Labs) into a substantial initiative with dedicated staff and growing grant activity, the need for distinct web presence and clearer communication about the relationship between the two entities became apparent.

The website discussion covered technical requirements, design approaches, content organization, and budget allocation for development. Creating separate but linked websites would help clarify for different audiences what each organization did, while maintaining transparency about the partnership and shared research infrastructure. The investment in web infrastructure reflected the professionalization and scaling of both GiveWell's traditional work and the newer Open Philanthropy Project.

RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN GIVEWELL, GOOD VENTURES, AND OPEN PHILANTHROPY PROJECT FUNDING

The board engaged in extensive discussion about the evolving relationship between GiveWell, Good Ventures, and funding for the Open Philanthropy Project. This 19-minute discussion represented a critical strategic conversation about governance, funding responsibilities, intellectual property, operational structure, and decision-making authority as the Open Philanthropy Project transitioned from a research initiative within GiveWell to what would increasingly function as a distinct but related entity.

Key issues likely included: clarifying which organization employed Open Philanthropy Project staff; determining appropriate cost allocation between GiveWell traditional work and Open Philanthropy Project; establishing protocols for grant decision-making; defining ownership of research outputs and intellectual property; and setting boundaries around independent versus collaborative decision-making.

The complexity of this discussion reflected the unusual nature of the arrangement. Good Ventures was simultaneously a major funder of GiveWell's operations, the primary partner and beneficiary of Open Philanthropy Project research, and a user of office space shared with GiveWell staff. Cari Tuna served as both President of Good Ventures and Treasurer of the Clear Fund Board, creating both synergies and potential governance complications requiring careful navigation.

ORGANIZATIONAL CONTEXT

This December 2014 meeting occurred during a pivotal period of transition and growth. GiveWell had established itself as the leading charity evaluator for evidence-backed global health and development interventions. The organization's recommendations influenced tens of millions of dollars in giving annually. Staff had grown substantially, the San Francisco relocation was complete, and operational systems had matured.

Simultaneously, the Open Philanthropy Project represented an ambitious expansion into new territory: research on causes and interventions that might have greater impact than proven cost-effective global health work but that required different evaluation methodologies and longer time horizons. The Project was increasingly functioning as a partnership between GiveWell and Good Ventures rather than simply another research area within GiveWell.

This dual identity created both opportunities and challenges. The organizations needed to clearly communicate their relationship to external audiences, ensure appropriate governance and accountability structures, allocate costs fairly, manage potential conflicts of interest, and maintain quality and focus in both traditional and new research areas. The discussions at this board meeting addressed many of these fundamental questions, setting the stage for the eventual formal separation that would come in later years.

The website discussion exemplified these challenges and the organization's approach to addressing them: create clarity through distinct but linked presences, maintain transparency about relationships and funding, and invest in professional infrastructure to support both traditional and innovative work. The budget approval reflected confidence that the organization could successfully execute on this complex dual mission while maintaining financial sustainability.
