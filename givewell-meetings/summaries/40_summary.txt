Episode 40: Board Meeting - February 27, 2020

Meeting Details:
- Date: February 27, 2020
- Duration: 1 hour 42 minutes
- Format: Video conference
- Chair: Tim Ogden

Summary:

This early 2020 board meeting took place just before the COVID-19 pandemic would fundamentally alter organizational operations. The meeting focused on reviewing 2019 achievements, setting 2020 priorities, and introducing new leadership while also exploring alternative approaches to board decision-making and engagement.

Leadership Additions and Organizational Structure:

A significant development was the presence of Buddy Shah, identified as Board Advisor and incoming Managing Director for GiveWell. Buddy would start in July 2020 and lead the organization's exploration of institutional influence—one of GiveWell's three major strategic priorities. His addition represented a major investment in expanding GiveWell's scope beyond individual donors to potentially influencing government aid agencies, large foundations, and multilateral institutions.

Also participating as Board Advisor was Norma Altshuler, identified as a prospective member of The Clear Fund's Board, signaling potential future board expansion.

2019 Review and Achievements:

Elie Hassenfeld led a comprehensive review of 2019 progress across all organizational domains, with particular emphasis on research and outreach developments.

Research Staff Management and Recruitment:
GiveWell had successfully expanded its research team with two senior researchers starting in late 2019, with one already managing a team of two. However, recruitment efforts through the American Economic Association job market (December 2019-February 2020) had yielded disappointing results despite receiving many more high-quality applications than in previous years. Candidates had underperformed on work trials, and GiveWell expected to make only 0-1 offers from that recruitment cycle.

The organization was pivoting its recruitment strategy, identifying individuals with backgrounds in evidence-based policy (using evidence to inform policy decisions) as particularly promising candidates based on successful recent hires. This more targeted approach aimed to find candidates who could contribute quickly based on relevant experience.

Donor Engagement:
The 2019 mini metrics report revealed important insights about GiveWell's donor base and money moved. While detailed numbers weren't specified in the minutes, the discussion informed strategic planning for outreach expansion.

Donor surveys had been conducted to better understand donor motivations, satisfaction, and potential for increased engagement. These surveys would guide refinements to GiveWell's messaging and donor cultivation strategies.

Operations Team Development:
Whitney Shinkle's operations team had continued to mature, with two Operations Associates added in 2019 enabling the team to take on more responsibilities than previously possible. The operations function was now running smoothly with almost no CEO involvement required—a significant change from earlier years when operations had been problematic and demanded substantial senior leadership attention.

Strategic Priorities for 2020 and Beyond:

GiveWell's three major strategic goals remained:

Finding More Cost-Effective Giving Opportunities:
The organization aimed to significantly increase room for more funding through two approaches:
- Increased scale at similar cost-effectiveness: Continue evaluating opportunities similar to current top charities
- Broader scope at greater cost-effectiveness: Explore opportunities beyond traditional GiveWell criteria, particularly in policy arenas

Research capacity and capability remained the biggest bottleneck. Elie's top priority continued to be recruiting and managing senior researchers who could contribute as individual contributors or managers.

Increasing Money Moved:
The goal of reaching $100 million in annual money moved (excluding Open Philanthropy) by giving season 2022 remained on track. In 2019, GiveWell had:
- Hired Jim Bobowski as Director of Marketing to lead mass marketing efforts
- Raised over $1 million in counterfactual donations through the major gifts program
- Conducted homepage revamp and podcast advertising that likely drove $500,000-$2 million in additional donations

For 2020, ambitious plans included:
- Increasing podcast advertising spend from $150,000 to $500,000 (contingent on finding quality opportunities and matching funds)
- Spending $500,000 on other paid advertising channels targeting 1.5x first-year tracked return on investment
- Major gifts team aiming to increase giving among existing donors by $5-7 million

Institutional Influence:
This area remained early-stage, with Buddy Shah set to lead the work starting in July. While tentative goals had been set for 2020, they were expected to evolve as the team refined its approach to influencing institutional funders.

Board Decision-Making and Engagement:

A notable agenda item was Whitney Shinkle's facilitation of a discussion on alternative decision-making approaches the board might adopt. This meta-level conversation reflected thoughtful consideration of how the board could most effectively engage with GiveWell's strategic direction.

The discussion likely covered:
- The balance between board oversight and management autonomy
- Appropriate levels of board engagement in different types of decisions
- How to make board meetings most productive and strategic
- Ways to leverage board expertise while avoiding micromanagement

This conversation demonstrated organizational maturity—willingness to examine and potentially improve fundamental governance processes rather than simply accepting established patterns.

Organizational Confidence and Capability:

Elie's assessment was notably positive: "Overall, GiveWell's nearly 40-person team is more capable than it has ever been. We're making progress in every single area."

Specific areas of progress highlighted:
- Operations had added two Operations Associates enabling expanded capacity
- The top charities team was running smoothly with minimal CEO involvement
- Individual researchers were actively working to find new giving opportunities
- Outreach team was building capability and running strategic experiments

This represented a significant evolution from just a few years earlier when multiple organizational areas required constant senior leadership attention to function adequately.

2020 Plans and Dashboard:

The meeting included reference to comprehensive planning documents (Attachment C) that outlined GiveWell's goals, subgoals, and objectives across all domains. The "Dashboard" provided a systematic view of organizational and department-level goals, domain subgoals, 2020 key projects, and detailed work plans.

This structured planning approach represented sophisticated organizational management—clear goal-setting, defined success metrics, and systematic tracking of progress. The waterfall structure (organizational goals → domain goals → subgoals → projects → detailed plans) ensured alignment between day-to-day work and ultimate mission.

Administrative Matters:

The board unanimously approved the minutes from the November 11, 2019 meeting, maintaining the standard practice of documenting decisions and discussions for transparency and accountability.

Looking Forward:

The meeting concluded with GiveWell well-positioned for 2020 execution across all strategic priorities. The organization had:
- Strong research capacity with plans for continued hiring
- Established outreach operations with clear growth metrics
- Smooth-running operations enabling organizational focus on mission delivery
- New leadership (Buddy Shah) joining to lead institutional influence work
- Sophisticated planning and tracking systems
- A board considering how to optimize its own effectiveness

The tone was one of confidence tempered with realism about the substantial work ahead. GiveWell had built strong foundations and was executing systematically on its vision to maximize impact on global health and poverty alleviation.

Unknown to participants, this would be the last "normal" board meeting before the COVID-19 pandemic would force significant operational adaptations and strategic reconsiderations. The strong organizational foundation built over preceding years would prove crucial for navigating the challenges ahead.
