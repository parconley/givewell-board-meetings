GiveWell Board Meeting Summary - Episode 15
Meeting Number: 17
Date: December 20, 2011
Duration: Not specified

OVERVIEW
This board meeting reviewed GiveWell's strong financial position and plans for substantial organizational expansion in 2012. The meeting focused on growth metrics showing dramatic increases in influence, the successful publication of two deeply-researched top charities, and the launch of GiveWell Labs with $1 million pre-committed. The organization projected a small surplus for 2012 while planning significant staff expansion.

FINANCIAL SITUATION - STRONG POSITION WITH EXPANSION PLANS
GiveWell's financial position was strong with a projected small surplus for 2012, though future expansion would require continued fundraising. As of December 15, 2011, projected expenses from November 2011 through December 2012 totaled approximately $632,677 against projected revenues of $636,500, creating a modest surplus.

The organization held reasonable cash reserves and could expand without immediately needing new revenue sources. However, projected 2013 expenses of $584,356 (based on reaching 8 staff by mid-2012) would create a funding gap of $100,000-$200,000 depending on whether William and Flora Hewlett Foundation support continued. Some 2012 revenue ($40,000) was one-time and would not repeat.

GiveWell planned to expand from 5 staff (as of mid-January 2012) to at least 8 staff during 2012. The expansion plan anticipated one new full-time hire or two part-time hires starting February 2012, plus two additional full-time hires starting July 2012. Timing reflected that most promising prospects were college seniors available starting summer. The organization felt confident it could productively use more staff given increasing influence, access, and research depth.

The budget allocated $20,000 for field visits ($15,000 for charity site visits, $5,000 for other travel to meet with people who could contribute to research). This represented an increase from $10,000 spent on two field visits in 2011 (three staff to Malawi, two to Delhi), reflecting improved access to relevant people and opportunities to travel for research purposes.

EXCESS ASSETS POLICY
GiveWell maintained an "excess assets" policy specifying that once unrestricted assets surpassed a certain level, the organization would grant excess funds to top charities rather than continue holding them. This policy ensured donors need not worry about room for more funding - when GiveWell had more than it could productively use, the result would be more grants to the best charities identified.

The policy aimed to hold a reasonable level of assets providing stable financial situation, ability to plan, and capacity to take outstanding opportunities (particularly hiring), while granting out anything beyond this need.

GROWTH IN INFLUENCE - DRAMATIC INCREASES
GiveWell's influence grew substantially in 2011 across multiple metrics:

Donations: Donations to top charities tracked through the website were up 200-300% in 2011 versus 2010 (which were up 200% versus 2009). As of December 15, 2011, GiveWell had tracked $543,115 through the website and $1,383,747 in overall money moved. The organization projected 2011 totals of approximately $1.4 million through the website (compared to $400,000 in 2010) and approximately $2.5 million overall (compared to $1.6 million in 2010). These figures excluded $1 million in committed funding for GiveWell Labs.

The growth was robust beyond just dollar amounts: the number of donations also increased 200-300% in 2011 versus 2010 (after growing only 50% between 2010 and 2009). Website traffic showed growth exceeding 100% in 2011 versus 2010.

Given continuing significant growth, basic outreach efforts, and substantially younger donor base than typical, GiveWell expected these figures to rise significantly in the future.

Credibility: The organization gained credibility among academics and funders partly due to growing donation impact, partly from improved networks, and partly from specific work including substantial analysis of Japan March 2011 relief effort funding needs and finding major errors in World Health Organization cost-effectiveness estimates.

Access: GiveWell's ability to get charities and scholars to engage improved significantly, providing access to more information requiring capacity to analyze and incorporate into research.

RESEARCH PROGRESS - TWO OUTSTANDING TOP CHARITIES
Since the August 2011 board meeting, GiveWell focused overwhelmingly on finding and researching top charities. The organization published new recommendations and research on November 29, 2011, featuring two top charities both researched in greater depth than any previous recommended charity and both considered outstanding.

Since publication, focus shifted to website optimization in response to increased traffic and feedback, reaching out to past donors for updates and discussions, and aiming for daily blog posts through calendar year end. In January, the organization would begin annual self-evaluation.

Research depth increased substantially for 2011 top charities, including extended pre-recommendation field visits and detailed original cost-effectiveness analysis (no longer relying on WHO estimates after finding substantial problems).

GIVEWELL LABS LAUNCH
GiveWell launched GiveWell Labs, a new research initiative aimed at serving donors capable of larger gifts and finding better giving opportunities generally. The initiative would be open to any giving opportunity regardless of form or sector, distinct from (though overlapping with) core charity-finding work.

$1 million had been pre-committed to GiveWell Labs. The research process would potentially involve investigations into many causes not previously covered. GiveWell made little progress on Labs in 2011 aside from launch but expected it to be a major priority for 2012.

The Labs initiative reflected new contacts with several "megadonors" (people with reasonable chance of making extremely large gifts of $1 million or more in reasonably near future) made in 2011, including Good Ventures. These contacts made it both more possible and more important to produce research useful to major philanthropists.

POSSIBLE PRIORITIES FOR 2012
The board reviewed several possible priorities for 2012:

Primary priorities: GiveWell Labs development; finding more top-rated charities by focusing on most promising interventions (vaccines, nutrition, tuberculosis control, neglected tropical disease control); finding more top-rated charities through open-ended investigation (along lines of 2011 process)

Additional priorities under consideration: More marketing/outreach effort, particularly at former employer (as first step in financial sector outreach); promoting GiveWell on college campuses as long-term strategy for cultivating employees and donors; website improvements; revisiting goal of having work evaluated externally and systematically

ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY AND MANAGEMENT
GiveWell's ability to hire and manage talent improved. In summer 2011, the organization worked productively with a 7-person team including two temporary hires. Staff was happy with productivity and management overhead despite expanded team size. As of mid-January 2012, the organization would have 5 people on staff, all with strong and substantial track records contributing to GiveWell.

The organization felt it developed good processes for identifying and managing new talent, supporting planned expansion.

STRATEGIC POSITIONING
This meeting demonstrated GiveWell at an inflection point - experiencing dramatic growth in influence while maintaining financial discipline and planning measured expansion. The 300% increase in money moved, coupled with launch of GiveWell Labs serving major donors, positioned the organization to operate at multiple scales simultaneously.

The commitment to excess assets policy showed continued focus on organizational mission over institutional growth, ensuring donated funds either supported operations productively or went directly to top charities. The substantial investment in field visits and deeper research reflected maturing research processes moving beyond desk research toward comprehensive due diligence including on-the-ground verification.
